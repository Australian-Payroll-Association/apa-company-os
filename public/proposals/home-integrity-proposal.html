What we heard

Where you are today

Home Integrity is a building and pre-purchase inspection business in Perth: 24 people, 160 to 180 inspections a week, scheduled by region, with inspectors on the road working from their iPads. The stack works, but it works hard against you:

  • HubSpot is home base. Deals are the source of truth for jobs, Data Hub wires the tools together, and the team lives in it.
  • Zuper is the weak link. It runs scheduling, and workflows break, servers go down, and fixes take a long time. The team is burnt out on it, and you are already unhooking reliance on it piece by piece.
  • The data behind scheduling is scattered. Inspector information sits partly in HubSpot, partly in Zuper, partly on spreadsheets. Employee records just moved to Employment Hero. Reports live in Safety Culture, invoicing in Xero, calls in Echo.
  • Leave is handled twice. An inspector requests leave in Employment Hero, then someone has to remember to block those days out in Zuper so no jobs land on them.
  • Scheduling itself is tribal knowledge. Regions, inspector capabilities, referral-partner priority, urgency: it all lives in the schedulers' heads. There is no SOP, because the nuance never fit into one.

Keeping it all talking to each other is close to a full-time job, the whole stack runs around A$10,000 a month, and you have lived through one big-bang migration already. Nobody wants a repeat of that.

The idea

The scheduling tool is the last piece, not the first

To schedule one inspection you need three things in one place: the inspectors (their regions, capabilities, calendars and leave), the jobs (your HubSpot deals), and the rules (the logic in your schedulers' heads). Today those live in four rented systems and a few clever people. Any scheduling tool built on top of that, ours included, would inherit the same fragility that makes Zuper painful.

So the real project is the foundation. We built Company OS, a company-database platform we run our own companies on, already covering CRM, HR, scheduling data, portals and reporting. Its data structures are modeled on HubSpot's, so your contacts, companies and deals sync in cleanly, and HubSpot keeps working exactly as it does today while you consolidate at your own pace. We stand up a central database you own, pull the employee spine out of Employment Hero and the jobs out of HubSpot, write the scheduling rules down at last, and then build the scheduling platform on ground that holds. No big-bang, no repeat of last time.

Risks & assumptions

The three that matter

1

If the scheduling logic resists being written down

You told us yourself: there is no SOP because the variables never fit one. If the rules stay in heads, no tool, and certainly no AI, can carry the load.

How we handle it

Documenting the scheduling workflows is a first-class deliverable of the foundation, done in working sessions with the schedulers themselves, not a template. SOPs come before software, and AI assist is only built on rules that exist in writing. If the map shows the logic is heavier than expected, the roadmap says so before you commit a dollar to the build.

2

If replacing Zuper mid-flight risks the operation

Zuper schedules 160 to 180 jobs a week today. A hard cutover that stumbles would hurt, and you have lived through a painful migration before.

How we handle it

No big-bang, ever. The new platform runs in parallel with Zuper, synced, until it has proven itself, and you cut over region by region on your call. Zuper is not cancelled until the last region is confidently off it.

3

If the data plumbing is messier than it looks

Your data lives across HubSpot, Zuper, Employment Hero, Safety Culture, Xero and Echo, and data quality is never fully visible until someone looks properly.

How we handle it

The systems map inventories every source before the build starts, and anything beyond the standard scope, heavy migration, cleanup, specialist connectors, is metered human-token work quoted before it starts. Nothing lands as surprise hours inside the fixed foundation fee.
