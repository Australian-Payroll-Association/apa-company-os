What we heard

Where you are today

You run a bricks-and-mortar retail business with an ecommerce arm, around 160 staff, mostly in stores, with about 20 in the back office. Your team is further along with AI than most:

  • LLMs are in daily use, and you already have some agentic workflows running.
  • Two quarters of groundwork done. You documented the workflows in your key departments to find the bottlenecks worth fixing.
  • Leadership is trained, the rest of the team isn't. Most of your people already use LLMs daily, but the structured training reached the leadership group and never filtered down.

And then it stalled. There's no in-house technical skillset to take the next step, and everyone is too stretched to self-teach. As you put it, the biggest problem is that everybody is under pressure all the time, under-resourced, never on top of the to-do list, always chasing. New projects land as one more thing rather than an opportunity. You've done the hard mapping. What's missing is the capability and the foundation to act on it.

The idea

It starts with owning your data

Most AI stalls for the same reason yours has: the data is scattered across tools that don't talk to each other, and every SaaS app you rent keeps your data in its database, not yours. If you ask a person to make a decision without the information in front of them, they can't. It is no different for AI. That is why so many teams are more productive with LLMs but stuck on what comes next.

So we stopped renting and started owning. We built Company OS, a company-database platform we run our own companies on, already covering CRM, HR, commerce, content, and reporting. That gives us a proven foundation to start from instead of a blank page. We map your data, stand up a central database you own, and build rapidly to your specifications on top of it, starting with the modules that fit a retailer. Where a tool you use today holds data you need, we can pull it in, so you consolidate onto a system you own at your own pace. The foundation is the head start; the build from there is shaped to how bStore actually works, on a clear meter once it is proven.

The opportunity

The question worth answering

What if the team you already have could get meaningfully more done without adding headcount, because the training finally reaches your store managers and back office, and a central database you own lets AI do the heavy lifting?

That is the goal you described, and the hard part is already done. The workflow documentation you spent two quarters on is exactly the input a database design and automation roadmap needs. What is missing is the capability to execute, and that is what the two tracks below deliver, in parallel, from August.

Risks & assumptions

The three that matter

1

If the 80% assumption doesn't hold for a retailer your size

The foundation rests on roughly 80% of what you need already existing in Company OS. If bStore is more bespoke than it looks, a fixed build would be wrong for both sides.

How we handle it

This is exactly what the mapping and roadmap sprint is for, and it carries the money-back gate. If the map shows a heavier job, the roadmap says so, and you can take the refund and stop there knowing exactly where you stand. The build only proceeds on a "go".

2

If building your data pipelines, or migrating off your current tools, is larger or messier than it looks

Data volume and quality in your POS, ecommerce platform, and back-office tools are what move a build like this, and neither is fully visible until someone looks properly.

How we handle it

The systems map inventories every source before the build starts, and anything beyond the standard scope, heavy migration, cleanup, specialist connectors, is metered human-token work quoted before it starts. Nothing lands as surprise hours inside the fixed foundation fee.

3

If the capability doesn't cascade past leadership

A database and a roadmap are only worth as much as the team that runs them. If AI stays with leadership again, nothing changes on the floor.

How we handle it

That is why certification is a co-primary track, not an afterthought. Thirty to forty of your people build real capability at their own pace, the training day puts them at the controls, and the leader dashboard lets you see the cascade happen. The whole point is the whole business, not just the leadership group.
