The starting point

You know where you are, which is most of the battle

About 70 people across two arms: Westbridge, packaging commercial property into managed trusts for investors, and Momentum Wealth, helping mums and dads buy and invest through buyer's agents, broking and property management. You run on Microsoft 365 and Copilot, on purpose, because familiarity and adoption matter more right now than chasing the shiniest tool. A handful of people have built real agents already: monthly journals for the accounting system, tax-return review, advice prep in the finance team.

The honest picture you painted: a two-tier business forming. The curious few are pulling ahead, the rest are drifting. Your FY27 plan already names the fix, and it is the right one. Get training and a consistent knowledge base in place. Appoint an AI and transformation lead to map processes and own the roadmap. Then build the higher-value agents from the top down. Underneath it all sits the piece nobody can see or touch, and the one that matters most: your data.

How we would look at it

Four moves, in this order

This mirrors what we talked through on the call. The sequence matters more than any single piece.

1
Move one  ·  coach the lead, build the roadmap

Turn your AI and transformation lead into a product manager for AI

Your lead probably did not come up as a product manager, and that is the skill the role needs. We link with them directly, help them set the strategy, and run a working session with your management team to build the actual roadmap: which processes, which agents, in what order, and what a human still owns at each step. We would run this in person if the timing works, so the team walks out with a roadmap rather than a reading list.

2
Move two  ·  educate the tiers

Let people self-select how far they go, and prove it

You said it yourself: push everyone down one path and you waste the curious and lose the rest. So we do not. Your management tier takes the AI Officer certification, the mindset for running an AI program. Your builders, the ones already shipping agents, take the AI Engineer certification and its 18 protocols. Certification here is proof of capability, not attendance: every exercise is logged, so you can see who genuinely progressed, who got stuck on the data work, and which handful of people could lead AI inside the business. That last list is the whole point.

3
Move three  ·  escalate the data layer

The one to push forward now, not later

Your goal is exactly right: type a prompt, get back the clients who have invested five times with Westbridge but nothing in the last twelve months, and the products they hold. That is not a tooling problem, it is a data-model problem. We start from a company database template that covers roughly 80% of the shape, then structure the last 20% around how your world actually works, property, funds, investors, and migrate your scattered SaaS data into one place you own. We do not sell you a platform. You own the database, you own the code on top, and you own the training that keeps your people building on it. Everything else fails quietly if this is skipped, so we would escalate it rather than leave it as a later stage.

4
Move four  ·  engineers on tap

Keep building, without carrying a bench

Your team can handle the simple agents, and should. For the heavier data and application work you flagged, you get access to our AI engineers two ways. Buy human tokens in blocks and spend them as you go, with a tool that tracks the work shipped against the tokens used, so you see features out the door, not hours billed. Or, when the roadmap is big enough, place a dedicated AI engineer with you on a one-year contract who sits inside a wider engineering team, so you get the scale and the range, better at data, back end, front end, design, without the overhead of hiring it all in.

Why the order

The part most people skip

Stanford looked at the successful AI programs and found around 77% of the work is not AI. It is centralising data and fixing workflows. Skip it and you are where MIT now puts most of the market: under 2% seeing real ROI, having bought the tools and the boot camps but built no foundation.

You have already convinced yourself of the hard part, that the invisible thing is the important thing. This program is built so the visible wins, the agents and the trained people, land on a foundation that holds instead of fragmenting six months later.
