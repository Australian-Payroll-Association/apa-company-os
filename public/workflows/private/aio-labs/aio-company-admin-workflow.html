The one thing to understand first

You receive a company — you don't build one

Edge8 sets a company up for you before you ever sign in. In a single step the back office creates the company, an active contract with your seat cap, and your own manager seat, then emails you a join link. You claim that seat, sign in, and land in the admin panel.

Everything you do afterwards happens inside that fixed cap and is scoped to your company alone. You never create a company, set your own cap, or reach another company's people — those belong to Edge8. What's yours is the seat list and what you can learn from it.

Getting in

Signing in as a manager

There's no separate admin password. When you sign in, the panel confirms your manager role straight from the database — not from anything stored in your browser — and unlocks the admin view. Every action you take then re-checks that you belong to this company, so a copied link or an edited cookie gets you nowhere.

  1. You claim your manager seat from the invite email, the same way your team will.
  2. You sign in normally; the panel recognises your manager role and switches to admin mode.
  3. Each screen and button re-verifies your company on the server before it does anything.
Where the line sits

Managers get the company admin panel — the seat list, your company profile, and monitoring. Back-office tools (creating companies, editing contracts, cross-company views) stay with Edge8, even if you hold other roles.

Done once

Complete your company profile

The first thing the panel asks for is your company details. Your company name is already there; you confirm it and add contact details — address, phone, website. This is a one-time step: once you save it, the name is locked and any rename goes through Edge8. Contact details you can edit whenever they change.

The one screen you'll live in

Seats come in two kinds

The seat list is where you spend almost all your time. Before the actions make sense, the two kinds of seat do. A person needs a basic seat to learn at all; a Leadership add-on is an extra, stacked on top of a basic seat for the people who need it.

Seat type one

Basic seat

Included in your subscription · standard certifications
  • Everyone on your team needs one to learn.
  • Counts against your basic seat cap — the number on your contract.
  • Grants the standard AI certifications.
  • You invite by email; the person claims it to begin.

This is the seat almost everyone holds. Your cap is set by Edge8; you can buy more.

Seat type two

Leadership add-on

Billed on top · adds the Leadership certification
  • Adds the Leadership cert to someone who already holds an active basic seat.
  • Drawn from a separate Leadership capacity you buy on top.
  • One person can hold a basic seat and a Leadership add-on at once.
  • Add or remove it per person, whenever there's capacity.

You can only add Leadership to someone who has already joined and is active.

Why seats are counted in one place

Every invite, claim, and revoke goes through the platform's seat list, checked as a single atomic step. That's what stops two people claiming the last seat, and why your used-versus-cap number is always exact. The seat list — not the invite email and not the join link — is the one authority on who has access.

The three states a seat can be in

What each state means for a real person

Every row on your seat list is in exactly one of three states. These words mean the same thing in the panel and in this document.

Invited

PersonHas an email with a join link. No access yet — they haven't claimed the seat.
YouThe seat is reserved and counts against your cap. You can resend the email or revoke it.

Active

PersonHas claimed the seat and can learn. Their progress lives on their own account.
YouYou can add or remove a Leadership add-on, or revoke the seat to free it.

Revoked

PersonAccess removed. Their learning history stays with their account, untouched.
YouThe capacity returns to your pool and the row leaves your active roster.
Freeing a seat is instant and exact

Revoking removes the person's access and returns the capacity to your pool in the same atomic step — there is never a moment where the seat is counted twice or lost.

What a manager can do

Five actions, all on the seat list

Managing your team is these five moves, nothing more. Each says exactly what it does — and, just as usefully, what it will never do.

Invite

Does
Adds one or more email addresses to the seat list and sends each a join link.
Never
Lets you exceed your cap — an over-cap invite is refused before any email goes out.

Resend

Does
Sends the join email again for a seat that's still Invited.
Never
Works on a seat that's already Active or Revoked — there's nothing to resend.

Revoke

Does
Frees the seat and removes that person's access at once.
Never
Touches another company's seats — you only ever see and act on your own.

Add Leadership

Does
Grants the Leadership certification to an active basic-seat holder, using free Leadership capacity.
Never
Applies before the person has joined, or when there's no spare Leadership capacity.

Remove Leadership

Does
Removes the Leadership certification and returns the add-on seat to your pool.
Never
Affects their basic access — they keep learning exactly as before.

From invite to first lesson

How someone joins your team

An invite doesn't grant access on its own — the person has to claim it. The link names your company so they know the invite is real, and on an invite-only contract the address itself is the gate.

  1. You invite an email address — the seat becomes Invited and a join link goes out.
  2. Your teammate opens the link and lands on a page that names your company.
  3. They claim the seat by confirming their email address.
  4. The seat flips to Active and they can start learning right away.
  5. On an invite-only contract, the link alone is not a way in — the address must be on your seat list, so a forwarded link grants nothing.
Watch for: unverified accounts

Sometimes a company mail filter blocks the invite email. That person may instead sign up with a password, without ever confirming they own the address. Those accounts appear in a separate Unverified list for you to check each is a real employee before you trust them.

Capacity & billing

Buying more seats

When you run low, you choose how many basic and how many Leadership seats you need, right in the panel. Capacity becomes usable immediately, so you can invite the moment you buy — the invoice is handled separately.

For billing

There's no card to enter. Buying seats raises an offline invoice — the moment you add capacity it's usable, and Edge8 is notified to bill for it. Your contract term and renewal are set on your contract by Edge8, not in this panel.

Keeping an eye on progress

Monitoring your team

From the same panel you can see how your team is actually doing: a twelve-week rhythm of who's been active and their weekly momentum, each person's progress and scores per activity, and the work they've produced — submissions and artifacts. It's all read-only and scoped to your company: you can't see another company's learners, and you can't change anyone's grades.

When you need a hand Rolling out

Getting help

When something needs Edge8's attention, you can raise a support request for your company and follow its status without leaving the panel. This is rolling out behind a feature flag; the seat and monitoring workflows above are already live.

Three parties, one seat list

Who owns what

Edge8 — back office

Creates your company and contract. Sets your seat cap and your company name. Raises your invoices, and handles renames and renewals.

You — the manager

Own the seat list: invite, resend, revoke, add and remove Leadership. Complete the company profile once, buy more capacity, and monitor learning.

Your learners

Claim their seat, learn, and earn certifications. Their history stays with their own account, whatever happens to the seat.

One source of truth

Access is whatever the seat list says — not the invite email, not the join link, not anything in the browser. Every action re-checks it on the server, so the number you see is the number that's real.
