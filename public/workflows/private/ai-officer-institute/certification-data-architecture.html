Core, electives, and the capstone

A companion to the AI Program Plan, focused on the part that matters most: how a certification is structured in data. Every certification is a required Core of missions, a set of elective micro-sessions, and a capstone that unlocks only when both are done. AI Officer is the worked example.

The shape

One track. Core plus Electives. Then the capstone.

A learner works toward one certification track at a time (Leadership, AI Engineering, or AI Officer). Credit is earned the same way in every unit: watch the video, read the textbook, do the challenge in the AI Buddy, submit. The certification is what those units add up to.

Core · required

Missions, the backbone. For AI Officer this is 10 missions organised into two course groups you move through in order.

Electives · required to finish

Micro-sessions: small, one video, one textbook, one small challenge. Any micro-session can count toward the current certification, once.

Capstone · final

The proof-of-competence project. It stays locked until the Core and the Electives are both complete, then issuing the credential is automatic.

Core

The 10 missions, Generative then Agentic

The AI Officer Core is 10 courses, not 3. It splits into two groups, and the journey runs Generative first, then Agentic. The prototype label "Foundation 3/3" was only ever the first group, which is what made the whole certification look small.

The rule that broke once

The completion percentage must count Core and Electives together, and the completion denominator must stay fixed at all 10 courses. When the Generative group was hidden as "coming soon", the denominator shrank from 10 to 6, so students who finished the 6 Agentic courses were shown as done and the capstone opened, yet no credential ever issued (incident BE #942). Hiding a group is presentational only, it must never change what "finished" means.

Electives

Micro-sessions that count toward the certification

Micro-sessions are the electives. They are exactly like a normal session, only small, so the screens and the credit mechanic are ones the learner already knows.

  • Content: a video, a textbook, and a small challenge.
  • Identical UI: the micro-session screens match a standard session, so the flow is familiar.
  • Live option: the screen can show the upcoming live session. A learner signs up, gets a Zoom link, and joins, building a community around the topic.
  • Earning credit: the same as a standard session, complete the challenge in the AI Buddy and submit.

How many are required

10proposed

A target of 10 required micro-sessions per certification is proposed, not yet settled. It cannot be uniform as written: the AI Engineer Core is only 8 courses (E01 – E08), so the elective rule needs to be defined per track.

Credit rule

A learner can apply any micro-session to the certification they are currently working on, like taking an elective in college (a Leadership class can count toward an AI Officer certification). It applies to that certification once, the same micro-session cannot be reused for a later certification. This is marked in the database.

Tags

Two independent tag axes

Every micro-session carries two tags on two independent axes: which office it is about, and which discipline (certification) it most applies to. A session is one of each.

Session types

Three ways to learn, one credit loop

Standard sessions, micro-sessions, and coaching differ in size and setting, not in how credit is earned. In all three the learner completes a challenge in the AI Buddy and submits it.

Standard session

The existing full-length session, the baseline unit of the curriculum. Missions are built from these.

Micro-session

A small elective: one video, one textbook, one small challenge. Identical screens to a standard session, plus an optional live sign-up.

Coaching

A live community session. Bring a real AI challenge, get coached, and the recording is archived so others learn from it.

Coaching

Open Coaching: bring a challenge, get coached

Open Coaching mirrors the prototype already running at caiocoach.com/coaching (our own site), brought into the AI Officer Institute. Like micro-sessions, coaching has an Upcoming view and an Archive.

  • Cadence: every Thursday at 11:00 AM GMT+7. A second time for the US will likely be added later.
  • Sign up and submit: anyone can sign up and submit an AI challenge they are having, and the top topics get coached that day.
  • Open to all: attendees can listen in and ask questions.
  • Recorded and published: each session is recorded and automatically published to the archive.

Numbers to reconcile first

The codebase disagrees with itself on the other tracks. AI Engineer shows "6 sessions" in onboarding but 8 courses (E01–E08) in the launch config; the old certification mock still shows a stale 4 + 4 two-track shape; and a fourth certification, Business AI Essentials, exists in code but is not in the three-track plan. AI Officer's 10-mission shape is the one part that is consistent everywhere, so it is the safe anchor for this brief.

For review

Decisions to lock before build

  1. Elective count and scope. Is it 10 required micro-sessions for every certification, or a number set per track?
  2. Progress formula. How do Core and Electives combine into the single percentage on the Home card: equal halves, weighted, or Core gates then Electives?
  3. My Catalogue. It moves onto the main certification screen as the evidence of work. Which fields per entry: title, tags, score, time to complete, submission link?
  4. Foundations data. Confirm Time to Complete and Score as the first two data points surfaced, and where they are computed.
  5. Capstone gate. Does the capstone unlock on Core complete alone, or strictly on Core and all electives? Today it opens on Core alone.
  6. Cross-track credit. Confirm the database marking that stops a micro-session being reused for a second certification.
