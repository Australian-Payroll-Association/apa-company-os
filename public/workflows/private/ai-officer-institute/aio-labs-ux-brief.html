AI Officer — what to fix, in order

14 Aug 2026 · Live walkthrough of aiolabz.com as learner + team admin · 33 findings P0 ×3 P1 ×12 P2 ×14 P3 ×4 · Full detail in the companion report

The product is stronger than the finding count suggests. The learning core — mission → challenge → AI Buddy — is genuinely differentiated, and the design language is consistent. The problem is closure: nearly every flow opens cleanly and fails to finish.

Three things that need fixing first

1
The certificate is unreachable at the moment it is earned P0 The screen says "Congratulations! You're a certified AI Officer" with a CERTIFIED badge — and Download Certificate and Share on LinkedIn are both greyed out, with no tooltip and no stated reason. This is the product's payoff, and it dead-ends. To a learner it reads as "the certificate isn't real."
2
Sign-up never verifies identity — and the cost lands on the paying customer P0 Sign-up takes a 6-character password, no email verification, no terms consent. The consequence appears in the admin: "Accounts to review (2) — signed up with a password and did not verify their email … please check each is a real member of your team." One unverified account (ash@edge8.ai) is already Certified at 100%. For a certification product this is an integrity problem, not a convenience one.
3
"Buy seats" has no price P0 No unit price, currency, total, confirmation step, invoice history, payment method, or cancellation path. Helper copy says Edge8 invoices separately — so the control is a request, but it is labelled and styled as a purchase. Largest trust liability in the admin area.

Root cause: these counts are hand-written in copy rather than derived from curriculum data. Individually trivial; together they undercut a product that sells rigour. Cheap to fix, high credibility return.

Other high-impact gaps P1

  • Admins can diagnose but not treat. The roster shows 3 learners never started and one who has never logged in — with no nudge, reminder, bulk action, track assignment or deadline. Highest-leverage single change for renewal.
  • "You're all caught up 🎉" is shown with 8 missions outstanding, and its only CTA sends the learner out to the blog.
  • The blog is a stalled pipeline the product points at. Newest post 15 Apr 2026 (4 months old), footer reads "© 2025", 31 articles with no search or pagination, one article tagged STRATEGY in the list and REVENUE on its own page.
  • Coaching archive promises auto-published recordings it does not have — all five past sessions read "Coming soon."
  • No account settings exist at all — no name, password, notifications or timezone. Every session time is hard-coded GMT+7.

Recommended sequence

Wave 1 — make flows terminateShip the certificate artefact (public URL + PDF + share). Gate the celebration on real completion. Verify email at sign-up. Price or honestly relabel the seat purchase.
Wave 2 — one source of truthSingle progress service across Home / Certification / Admin. Derive every count from curriculum data. Fix the micro-session checklist against its own data panel.
Wave 3 — give the admin a leverMulti-select nudge with a last-nudged record. Role + seat type + track + target date at invite. Prevent seat exhaustion. Confirm destructive actions. Ship /settings, starting with timezone.
Wave 4 — close the funnelBlog search/sort (the Coaching archive pattern already exists — reuse it). One category vocabulary. Byline + one contextual CTA back into a mission. Rebuild onboarding as three capturing steps ending in "Start Mission 1".

One decision blocks the roadmap: is the certificate download blocked by an unmet requirement, or is the feature simply unbuilt?

If it is a requirement, Wave 1 is a state-logic fix this week and the celebration banner should never have appeared. If it is unbuilt, it is the next thing to build — and everything else waits.

Scope note. No accounts were created and no forms submitted — invite, Buy seats, Request Access and Remove were documented from their UI only, so their validation and error states are unverified. Responsive behaviour was untestable (fixed desktop viewport), so mobile comments are recommendations, not observed defects. One AI Engineer challenge was started to document the runtime; nothing was submitted and progress remains 0%.
