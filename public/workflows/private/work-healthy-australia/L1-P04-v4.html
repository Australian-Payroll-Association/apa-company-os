Process 04: Schedule the Service

Prerequisite
The Site Service calendar exists, carries the Site's operating hours, and has a Clinician or Practitioner assigned to it. Every Service Order carries an estimated period.
Trigger
A Service Order is raised for a Site, a booked Patient does not attend, or a Clinician or Practitioner becomes unavailable for a date range.
Urgent
A complaint or injury Order may be marked urgent by the Employer raising it, and only that kind may be. Urgent means someone is hurt or a situation is unsafe now, so the Order does not join the queue. It lands as an alert on the Day Sheet of the Clinician or Practitioner covering the Site the moment it is raised, and is seen today. An emergency is a different thing again: an ambulance and the emergency department, not an Order, which is the triage line every industry reference draws.
Rule
The Day Sheet is the day's booked sessions for one Clinician or Practitioner at one Site, read from the Site Service calendar. The urgent alert lands there because the person who owns the day is the only one who can rule how the day absorbs the case. Drawn for the Clinician; a Practitioner rules their own Day Sheet the same way inside their own Client organization.
Rule
Two ways to absorb it, and the day owner chooses. Push the day: every remaining session moves back one, and the day runs past the Site's hours into overtime where it must. Clear the day: every non-critical service on the sheet is rebooked to the next available slot through the normal search, and the freed time takes the case. What counts as critical is decided by the service, not by the person: another injury does not move, a screening does. Every moved Patient and their Employer contact are told at the moment of the move, the same way any booking is confirmed. Overtime needs no special object, because Process 09 bills time and a long day is simply more recorded time.
Open
Whether an Order raised by a case in Process 06 or by the clock in Process 08 may also be urgent. Today only the Employer marks it, at raise time, on a complaint or injury Order. Not ruled.
Output
An appointment on the Site Service calendar: a named Clinician or Practitioner, a Patient, and a slot of the estimated period inside the Site's operating hours. Booked as a placeholder until accepted.
Output
Where there is no capacity in the Site's hours, the Order is returned unbooked. Site, date and time cross the wall. Nothing else.
Output
A weekly no-show report of the Patients who did not attend, carrying the open Service Orders that need follow-up.
Rule
An open Service Order is never dropped, and it is never rebooked by default. Where Work Healthy Australia moves the date, the Order keeps a slot. Where the Patient does not attend, the Order releases its slot and waits on the no-show report until follow-up books it again.
Rule
The schedule is a plan, not a gate. It never decides how released time is used and never prevents a Patient being seen earlier than the slotted time.
Rule
A Service Order raised by the clock in Process 08, or by a case in Process 06, enters at the search with no Employer behind it. An Employer amending a booking they already hold enters the same way.
Rule
The slot search reads travel time as well as the Site's working hours. A slot the assigned person cannot physically reach is not a slot.
Rule
The estimated period comes from the client and the service together, not from the service alone, because the same service runs longer at some Sites. Where that resolution happens is below this level.
Rule
The Patient is entitled to be served under this Client organization when their work email address belongs to it. The Service Order carries that check, so the door in 05a verifies who a person is rather than whether they belong.
Rule
Confirmation goes to both the named Employer contact and the Patient at the moment of booking, and the paperwork goes out before the day.
Rule
The Order carries a short check-in code from the moment it is raised, the way an airline issues a booking reference, so a walk-in with no booking still arrives holding one. It is a correlation key, not a credential. One code ties the Service Order, the appointment, the paperwork issued before the day, the consents given against it, and later the outputs, so any one of them pulls up all of them with certainty. A booking reference ties a reservation to checked luggage, a boarding pass and a screening status, and it still does not board anyone. Identity is a separate act, in Process 05a.
Rule
An Employer request that cannot be met is returned with a reason, the same way the no-slot alert is. A request falling outside the contracted working days is a different case from one that fits, and is returned as such.
Roles
Patients, Clinicians, Practitioners, Admin, Employers
