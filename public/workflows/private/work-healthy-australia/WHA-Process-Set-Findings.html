Work Healthy Australia process set: findings and proposals

Prepared by Edge8, 2 August 2026. A step by step comparison of the proposed process design against the capabilities held in IMS, and four proposals for sign-off.

Purpose

The process set describes how a signed agreement becomes an invoice, in eleven diagrams covering nine processes. Every step in it has been walked against IMS to establish one thing: whether the capability already exists, or whether it has to be built.

That matters because it is what the commitment to drop no existing capability gets tested against. A step marked Covered is a migration. A step marked New is a build. Nothing is assumed either way.

Eight of those steps were then examined a level further down, because each hides a sequence with its own decisions rather than a single action. Comparing at that level changed six of the answers, in both directions, and those corrections are carried in the table below rather than listed separately.

The comparison was made against the schemas of the three production databases: the live IMS database, the IMS version 2 schema, and the client portal. Alongside those sit the screens captured from the live system in June, and published practice from KINNECT, Jobfit, Sonic HealthPlus, OSHGroup and the occupational health standards of the Netherlands, Japan, the United Kingdom, Germany and Finland.

One qualification applies throughout and is not repeated in every row. A schema establishes what a system is built to hold. It does not establish how much of it is used in practice. The closing section sets out what would settle that.

One absence that accounts for three of the gaps

Three of the shortfalls above are the same shortfall seen from three directions. Nothing in IMS links a person to the services they are permitted to perform.

The consequence appears in three places. Credentials can be captured but cannot govern anything, so a lapse has no effect until somebody notices. The scheduling search can filter on working days, committed hours and travel, but cannot exclude a clinician who is not certified for the service being booked. And a determination that needs a second signature before release has no way to identify who is qualified to give it.

That is one piece of work rather than three, and it unlocks more than anything else on the list. It is worth naming separately because at first reading it looks like three unrelated items in three different processes.

Four proposals for sign-off

Each of these is put forward with a recommendation already made, so that a decision is a confirmation rather than an open question. Where there is no response, the recommendation stands and the design proceeds on it.

#What we proposeIf they disagree
1Two clinical roles. One employed by Work Healthy Australia working across every Client organization, one contracted to a single Client organization. We could not evidence the second anywhere in the current estate, so we may have invented a distinction that does not existCollapse to one clinical role. This is the smaller and simpler design, and it removes the scope gate from five diagrams
2Review before release is decided by the service, not the person. A determination that crosses the wall gets a second signature from someone holding the right registration. A health check does notThey name which services need it. The mechanism does not change, only the list
3Barriers to recovery are recorded, and separately marked resolved, following standard return-to-work practiceThey describe how a stalled case is actually recorded today, and we match it
4Build the clock. Requirements sit on the Site and the Role, dates raise the work, and due and overdue are visible per Site. This is what turns a series of requested services into a monitoring programme, and it is the single largest build on the listIf the commercial intent is to keep selling individually requested services, the clock defers and nothing else in the design moves

The fourth is the only one that turns on commercial intent rather than clinical practice. It decides whether the system supports a monitoring programme or a series of individually requested services, and it is the largest single item of build on the list.

What would sharpen the migration estimate

Every step marked Covered above rests on the database schema, which establishes that IMS is built to hold the capability. A single read only query returning the row count of each table would establish how much of it carries data and is still written to today.

That would refine the effort and sequencing of the migration. It does not affect the design, which was drawn from the work itself and tested against published practice before IMS was examined at all. Access can follow at whatever pace suits, and nothing waits on it.

Three areas in particular would benefit from that confirmation: whether the approval step recorded against assessment records is routinely used, whether the return to work barrier records carry data, and whether the workforce survey tables are current or belong to a superseded product.
