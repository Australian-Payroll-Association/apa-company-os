MVP product plan: the pilot, ready to build

Written 2 August 2026 for the sessions that will build it. This is the build authority for phase 1, the pilot. It is MVP-Implementation-Plan.md with every correction from MVP-Plan-Evaluation.md folded in as settled design, so a building session reads this document and does not need to reconcile the two. Where this plan and a process diagram disagree, the diagram wins and this plan is corrected. Everything here is proposed until it runs and produces a record.

1. What this document is, and how to use it

The programme has three phases. Phase 1 is the pilot: one client runs pre-employment screening and complaint or injury evaluation end to end on the new record, from signed agreement to paid invoice, with the employer wall live and the migration pipeline proven on a named slice of real data. Phase 2 is current operations matching: everything the existing systems do today runs on the new record, with the history migrated and reconciled. Phase 3 is the future state: the clock is live, check-ups are raised from dates, and the model is differentiated from anything in the industry. The master plan (Master-Product-Plan-Three-Stages.md, which calls the phases stages) carries phases 2 and 3, the full table inventory, and the migration acceptance criteria. This document carries phase 1 only.

A building session should read, in order: this document, then the L1 process page for the step being built (the version 4 pages are the specification), then the L2 page for any engine in that step. The published set at /artifacts is the fastest way in. Never hand-edit a diagram; edit the generator under tools/ and rebuild.

The build happens in eleven ordered steps, numbered 0 to 10, some split into parts. A step is done when its exit criteria pass through the verification harness, witnessed where the step says so. Durations are deliberately absent; the steps are sized so the people doing the work can estimate each one from its page.

2. Rules that bind

Settled during design and evaluation. A building session does not reopen these.

  1. Five roles. The Patient sees their own record only, through links and codes, never a sign-in. The Clinician is employed by Work Healthy Australia and works across every assigned Client organization. The Practitioner is contracted to exactly one Client organization, and that scope is never widened. The Employer receives outputs and never clinical detail. Admin does setup, accounts and authorization, with no clinical detail.
  2. The wall. Only the determination and the other named release kinds cross to the Employer. Consent gates collection, not release, because the Employer commissioned the evaluation.
  3. Consent is once per Client organization, checked on later visits, held against the version of the wording signed. The door verifies consent; the paperwork before the day collects it.
  4. Identity. No clinical record is created, opened or amended until the person attending is confirmed to be the Patient named on the Service Order. On a mismatch, no clinical record exists for the person who attended; the Service Order still closes with a named state, the Employer still receives a completion-failed notice carrying no reason, and the minutes still enter the debrief.
  5. A restriction is measured, never asserted.
  6. One clock, stated precisely. Process 08 is the only process that raises clinical work from a date, and it is not built in phase 1. Commercial cadences, the report cadence and the invoice acceptance hold, are configuration on the service agreement, run by scheduled jobs or by Admin from a view, and are not the clock.
  7. Billing is time entry. There is no billable event. The debrief is the time entry, the day locks before invoicing, and the invoice traces to locked minutes. A late cancellation and a failure to attend are records, never charges.
  8. The Patient is never onboarded as an employee. Applicant and employee are two person records by design. There is no worker roster; the Employer nominates who receives a service, per Service Order.
  9. No build on the existing OccuSpan codebase. This is a new repository and a new, dedicated Supabase project sharing nothing with any existing project.
  10. Writing. Plain English everywhere, including error messages and gate refusals. Business objects by their names: Client organization, Site, Department, Role, Patient, Clinician, Practitioner, Employer, Admin, Service Order, Site Service calendar. Never the acronym for Work Healthy Australia in anything external. Every closed gate produces a plain sentence saying what is wrong in the reader's own nouns.

3. Scope

Processes in scope, as drawn in version 4: 01 Setup Workplace, 02 Onboard / Offboard, 03 Define the Role Requirements, 04 Schedule the Service, 05a admission, 05b render and determine, 05c review and release, 09 Report to Invoice.

Subprocess pages in scope: 01-RD requirement defaults, 02-CR credentials, 03-MS measure the demands, 04-SL slot search, 05a-CN consent set, 09-DB the debrief. One more page is drawn before step 4b: the paperwork pack subprocess (candidate 6 on L2-Candidates-For-Decision.md), because it is where consent is actually collected and step 4b's exit criterion has no acceptance authority without it.

Out of scope, registered not dropped: processes 06, 07 and 08; the Treatment box; bulk migration; Employer self-service beyond raising a Service Order and receiving outputs; organization parents above the Client organization; standing alerts. Each has a home in phase 2 or 3.

The two pilot services: pre-employment screening, and complaint or injury evaluation. A pilot complaint or injury evaluation ends at the released determination and its mitigation plan; the persistent case is phase 2, and the bridge for a real unfit person is in step 10.

4. What changed from the implementation plan

The evaluation (MVP-Plan-Evaluation.md) found ten things; this plan folds in the corrections. Recorded here so the delta is traceable, then never repeated: the sections below simply state the corrected design.

  1. Two wall interaction tables added, so the Employer can raise requests and sign documents without any grant outside the wall schema.
  2. Two catalog tables added: credential-to-service mappings and per-service consent requirements. Four grant rulings recorded in section 6.
  3. The wall's enforcement mechanism is named (section 5), and the wall gate test is reworked (section 8).
  4. Four date corrections: the 03 document review date writes the due-date ledger and is added to 08's enumeration in the diagrams; a calendar coverage-end due item; homes for the report cadence and acceptance hold; a supersession rule.
  5. The step order is corrected: steps 1 and 2 run in parallel, step 3 follows both; the consent set engine moves into step 4b.
  6. Steps 0, 3 and 4 are split into parts so each is estimable by one person who has read its page.
  7. Step 9 gains an entry criterion (row counts on the snapshot before the slice is confirmed) and a regression rule, and the obfuscation specification becomes a per-column policy.
  8. Step 10 gains the bridge into the current system for a pilot determination, and a finance owner for the dual invoicing period.
  9. Settlement fields on invoices, the identity mismatch rule stated for implementers (section 2, rule 4), and the environments stated precisely (section 9).

The table count becomes eighty-seven: thirty-three in core, thirty-eight in clinical, six in wall, five in billing, five in audit. All are created in the first migration set with row-level security, deny by default. No phase adds a table.

5. Architecture

Stack. Next.js App Router with strict TypeScript, one application serving four surfaces as route groups: Admin workspace, clinical workspace shared by Clinicians and Practitioners differing only in scope, Patient link pages with no sign-in, and the Employer portal. Supabase as the platform: Postgres with the six schemas, Auth for the four signed-in roles, Storage for evidence and released documents, scheduled functions for the timed jobs. Vercel, deployed only by git push through CI. Resend for email, an SMS gateway for the paperwork and confirmation messages. Playwright drives the verification harness.

The wall, mechanically. A Postgres role employer_wall is created in the first migration, granted to authenticator, with usage on the wall schema and nothing else, ever. Supabase's Custom Access Token hook stamps that role onto every Employer account's JWT at token issue, so an Employer's own session reaches the database as a role that structurally cannot read a clinical table. Reads that originate elsewhere cross into wall as views owned by a privileged role: the report and invoice from billing, booking status as site, date and time. Writes go the other way through the two interaction tables in section 4, processed by trusted internal code. Three hygiene conditions are step 0a exit checks and stay checks forever: no default privilege may ever grant employer_wall anything outside wall; storage policies carry the same role discipline as tables; and the module boundary in the application keeps Employer routes on the Employer client factory, with the grant as the backstop for a routing mistake, not a substitute for the boundary.

Authorization is one guard and two tables. Every server action calls the same guard, which answers from role_capabilities for data scope and user_service_grants for function scope. Row-level security policies join the same tables. No role names in code, no per-page checks. Who can read a clinical note is one row lookup.

Patient links are a fifth path and are named as one. patient_access_links rows carry a single-purpose token, a scope, and an expiry. The link endpoints run server-side under a dedicated restricted role, validate the token, and may touch only the rows the link's scope names. Tokens are stored hashed. Every use writes the audit log. No Supabase Auth user is ever created for a Patient. This path bypasses the signed-in guard by construction, which is why its discipline is written here rather than assumed.

Documents. Release documents, the report of services rendered, and invoices render server-side to PDF, stored in the wall bucket, issue logged in release_log_entries. Evidence for Role requirements documents, signed consent files and external certificates live in clinical buckets keyed to their records.

Scheduled work is small. Four jobs, all idempotent, all logging delivery to notifications: issue the paperwork pack when a booking confirms, retry undelivered notices against the fallback recipient, compile the weekly no-show report, and issue held invoices when their acceptance date arrives. These are jobs, not the clock, per rule 6.

Grant rulings, settling what the pages left open. A grant row carries its source credential, so suspending one credential leaves a grant standing where another still covers the service. Review grants are derived, not separate: when 02 confirms a credential whose registration matches a service's reviewer registration in service_types, a review row is written to user_service_grants, and the lapse cascade covers review grants exactly as it covers perform grants. That keeps the 05c page's rule, the reviewer is qualified by registration, while letting the gate answer from one table. Unlock-a-day is not derived from credentials; it is an Admin-granted row with a named grantor, and 02 carries the granting step. The slot search evaluates the grant as of the slot's date, so a credential already known to expire before the proposed booking excludes that slot.

Date rulings. Publishing a Role requirements document writes its review date to due_items, and process 08's enumeration on the diagrams is corrected to include it (a generator edit and rebuild, done before step 3a exits). Loading a year of working days writes a coverage-end due item, so the calendar cannot run out silently. Any step that replaces a date-carrying record, a credential renewed, a document republished, closes the prior due item; the harness tests supersession wherever an engine touches a date. Credential expiries write due_items at confirmation, mirrored, with the renewal superseding the old row.

8. The verification harness

Built in step 0b because it is the product's admission ticket. Four parts.

  1. Role-stamped passes. Playwright drives every capability end to end through real sign-ins as each role that touches it, no bypass header, one stored evidence record per pass: who, what, when, against which build.
  2. The safety gate. For every table a step touches, a fixture from the wrong organization attempts the read and the write. Pass means failure: closed, with a plain sentence, before any work.
  3. The wall gate, two tests. First, structural: the employer_wall role's session cannot select from any schema but wall, asserted from the grants, one query. Second, content: every release document kind carries an allowlist contract, owned and signed by the privacy owner, and every Employer response is asserted against the contract for its kind. The old idea of mechanically diffing column names against the clinical schema is dropped; shared names like identifiers and timestamps made it either noise or theatre, and the real leak risk is what 05c writes into a release row, which only a contract can test.
  4. The witnessed demo. Per step where named, the capability is walked against its process page by the Work Healthy Australia person who does that job, and the sign-off is recorded. The judges are the people who hold the knowledge.

9. The migration rehearsal, environments and operations

The slice, proposed and pending counts: the tblJobs cluster, CertOfCapacity with TreatmentDuties, and ConsentSignature, archive-flagged rows included on purpose. The slice is confirmed only after the query pack runs against the snapshot; a slice whose tables turn out sparse is re-named, because a rehearsal against empty hazards proves nothing and reports confidence.

The obfuscation policy is per column, not blanket. Written with the custodian, signed by the privacy owner, in four classes: direct identifiers tokenised deterministically and reversibly; free text replaced; dates shifted by a consistent per-person offset so intervals and orderings survive; flags, enumerations and scores preserved, because the mapping engine's state reconstruction branches on them. The privacy claim, stated as what the pipeline keeps: no person is identifiable in the transform environment or in any AI tooling context. The map is generated and held in the custodian's environment and never leaves it.

Environments. One dedicated Supabase project. Pilot production is the project's main branch, where point-in-time recovery applies from day one; development and staging are persistent branches with seeded fixture clients. Schema migrations are versioned files applied through CI in order, never by hand against production. .env.example carries every variable with a one-line comment from the first commit. Deploys are git push to main through CI; no command-line deploys. A restore is tested before step 10.

The pilot's two seams, named. When a pilot determination is not fit or fit with restrictions, the ongoing case runs in the current system, because process 06 is phase 2. A named Work Healthy Australia person carries the determination across, and the act is recorded; the privacy owner sees this paragraph before go-live. And for the pilot period the practice invoices from two systems; a named finance owner reconciles until phase 2 ends the split.

10. Exit

Phase 1 is done when, all witnessed: a real applicant is screened and a real employee evaluated end to end through real sign-ins; review happened where the service required it, with the reviewer recorded; the Employer holds a release document and nothing else; the invoice went out from locked minutes, was held to its acceptance date, and its payment is recorded; no step of the service path read a legacy system; and the rehearsal slice round-tripped with its evidence in migration_reconciliations, reproduced by a second operator.

What exits with it: the runbooks per step, the stored role-stamped evidence, the signed witness record per process, the signed catalogs and content contracts, and the mapping engine and runbook that phase 2 scales.

11. Sign-offs and open items

The sign-off ledger. The privacy owner signs the release content contracts per kind, the obfuscation policy, the patient link surface, the retention clocks, and the bridge paragraph. Clinical governance signs the service catalog, the credential-to-service mappings, the consent requirements per service, and the restriction vocabulary. The custodian holds the obfuscation map and confirms the slice. Both owner roles must be Work Healthy Australia people, and both block step 10, with clinical governance also blocking the exits of steps 2 and 4b, which is earlier than the implementation plan had it.

Open items, unchanged and deliberately so. Consent per Client organization or per service type; whether a health check's result belongs to the Patient (if it ever does, the personal result is delivered through the patient link path, never through the wall schema, which is the Employer's grant); whether credential expiries belong in process 08; the anonymous survey's consequence; and the four standing proposals in WHA-Process-Set-Findings.md, including whether the two clinical roles collapse to one, which this schema absorbs as capability matrix rows either way. Each lands as rows or an already-present nullable column. None blocks phase 1.
