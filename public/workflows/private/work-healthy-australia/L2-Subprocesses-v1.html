L2 01-RD: Set the requirement defaults

Sits under Process 01, step: Set the requirement defaults on each Site and Role

Question it answers
Which rule wins when a requirement is set at more than one level, and what happens when two set different intervals for the same thing.
Answer
Most specific wins, and the shorter interval wins. The two are different rules and both are needed.
Rule
Requirements attach at three levels and are held separately, never merged at the point they are set. A Site rule says everyone here needs this. A Role rule says everyone doing this needs it. An intersection rule says this Role at this Site only.
Rule
The effective set is computed when a Service Order is raised, not when the rule is written. That is what lets a rule change without rewriting the history it has already resolved.
Rule
Where two layers set the same requirement, the most specific wins: intersection, then Role, then Site. Where two set different intervals for it, the shorter wins, and nothing may lengthen another's interval. A Site may demand a test sooner than the test's own validity. It may never demand it later.
Why it matters
Processes 04, 05b and 08 all resolve against this. An error here is silent and shows up as the wrong service being booked, or a due date that never arrives.
In IMS
The three attachment layers exist. A Site rule is `Entity_MedTest`, a Role rule is `CompanyRoleMedTest` and `CompanyRoleFunctionalTest`, and the intersection is the same tables carrying both a CompanyID and a CompanyRoleID. Client overrides sit in `CompanyLoadRating` and `CompanySwitchDemandRating`. What is absent is the resolution: nothing encodes most-specific-wins, and nothing lets a Site shorten a test validity that `MedTest.TimeLimit` sets. So the layers migrate and the precedence is built.
Roles
Admin

L2 02-CR: Confirm credentials and their expiry

Sits under Process 02, step: Confirm credentials and their expiry

Question it answers
Whether a credential is verified or merely recorded, and what happens to work already booked when one lapses.
Answer
Verified at the source where a public register exists, recorded against a held document where it does not. A lapse suspends the grant immediately, and any booking already made against it has to be re-assigned or returned.
Rule
Two verification paths, because the professions split. Physiotherapists, occupational therapists, doctors and nurses are registered with Ahpra, which publishes a register anyone can check, renews annually, and sets standards for recency of practice, continuing professional development and professional indemnity insurance. Exercise physiologists are not Ahpra-registered and are accredited through their own body. Where a register exists the check is made against it and the date of the check is recorded, not just the number.
Rule
Instrument certifications are course-based rather than registered, and are held as documents. Spirometry requires a course meeting the Thoracic Society standard. Audiometry requires training under AS/NZS 1269.4. Neither appears on a public register, so the evidence is the certificate.
Rule
The same cycle covers everything else with a date on it: professional indemnity insurance, criminal history and working with children checks, immunisations, first aid and resuscitation, and each client Site's own induction. Capture, verify, track, and hand the expiry onward.
Rule
A credential grants services, and only the services it covers. This is the function half of authorization, and it is why two Clinicians are not interchangeable in Process 04's slot search.
Rule
Every expiry is a date, so Process 08 watches it. Nothing in this process waits for one.
Rule
A lapse is not a warning. The grant suspends on the date, the person may no longer perform that service, and Orders already booked against them for it are re-assigned or returned. Discovering it at the door is the failure this step exists to prevent.
In IMS
Capture is there. `ProviderRegistration` holds the type, the state, the number and the expiry, `RegistrationType` names the kinds, and `Provider.PINumber` with `PIExpiryDate` covers indemnity. `SiteProviderNumber` holds the per-Site number. What is absent is everything after capture: no field records that a register was checked or when, no link from a credential to the services it permits, and no cascade when one lapses. `Users.Archive` and `UserRoles.ArchiveDate` suspend access by hand, on no date.
Roles
Admin, Clinicians, Practitioners

L2 03-MS: Measure the demands of each task

Sits under Process 03, step: Measure the demands of each task

Question it answers
Whether the measurement instruments run in series or in parallel, and what decides which of them run at all.
Answer
In parallel, after one screen. None of them reads another's output, so nothing forces an order. The only real sequence is screen first, then deep-dive on whatever the screen surfaced.
Rule
One screen always runs. ManTRA is scored across the five body regions for every task, because it is the only instrument that covers a whole task rather than one feature of it.
Rule
The deep-dive instruments are conditional and scoped, which is why they are not alternatives to each other. The NIOSH equation applies only where there is a lift or a carry, and needs the load, the heights and the distance. REBA scores a posture that is held or awkward, at a moment in the task. Force measurement applies where grip, push or pull is the demand. A task can trigger all three, one, or none.
Rule
Every instrument outputs a number on the same scale, which is the only thing this process requires of any of them. Adding or replacing an instrument changes nothing above this level.
Rule
Tasks roll up to the Role. The Role's demand for each measure is the envelope across its tasks, not the average, because a person has to be able to do the hardest one.
Output
The minimum ratings on the Role that the functional tests in 05b are compared against.
In IMS
Close to complete, and the strongest parity in the set. The task tree is `TaskHierarchy` with `tblJobs.ParentID`. The ManTRA screen is `tblJobs` itself, scored across five body regions for time, duration, cycle, repetition, force, speed, exertion, awkwardness, vibration and cumulative risk, against the `lktbl_ManTRA` lookups. The deep-dives are `tblJobManualHandling` with `LoadRating`, `tblJobPosturalDemands` with `lktbl_Posture`, and `tblJobGripDemands` with `lktbl_Grip` and `lktbl_Force`. The common scale is `lktbl_PhysicalDemands`, the risk rating is `lktbl_RiskAssessment` with probability, exposure and consequence, and the roll-up is the `Min` rating columns on `tblJobs`. Only the instrument selection step has no home, because IMS records what was measured and never why.
Roles
Clinicians, Practitioners

L2 04-SL: Search for a slot

Sits under Process 04, step: Search the Site Service calendar for a slot

Question it answers
The order the constraints are applied in, and what comes back when none of them leaves a window.
Answer
In series, narrowing, and each filter only ever removes. The opposite of the instruments in 03-MS, which are parallel because none of them reads another's output. Here every filter reads what the last one left.
Rule
The order is not arbitrary. Cheapest and most selective first: the Site's own working days, then its hours, then whoever holds the credential for this service, then their remaining hours, then travel from wherever they were before. Reversing it computes travel for people who were never eligible.
Rule
The estimated period is what the window is measured against, and it comes from the client and the service together.
Rule
No slot is a legitimate outcome. The Order returns unbooked, and Site, date and time are all that cross the wall with it.
In IMS
Four of the five filters exist. Working days and hours are `CompanyDaysOfService` with `DateFrequency` and its own start and finish dates. Committed hours are `UserRoster`, `UserRosterRecurrence` and `ProviderHoursPerWeek`. Travel is `UserTravelDuration`, and `IMSShift.TravelMinutes` records what it actually took. The estimated period is `CompanyApptDuration`, keyed to the company and the service. The filter that does not exist is the credential one, because nothing links a person to the services they may perform. So the search migrates with a filter missing from the middle of it.
Roles
Admin

L2 04-UR: Absorb an urgent Order

Sits under Process 04, step: the day owner rules how the day absorbs an urgent Order

Question it answers
What happens to a full day when an injured person is on their way in: who has the authority to reshape the day, by what test, and what happens to everyone already booked on it.
Answer
The day owner rules once, at the alert. Where overtime can hold every session, the day is pushed. Where it cannot, every non-critical service is rebooked through the normal search and the freed time takes the case. The urgent case is seen today under either ruling.
Rule
Only the Employer marks urgency, only on a complaint or injury Order, only at raise time. Urgent is not emergency. The industry triage line puts an emergency in an ambulance and the emergency department; an urgent Order is a person who must be seen today by the Site's own Clinician or Practitioner.
Rule
The ruling belongs to whoever owns the Day Sheet, because pushing a day into overtime and moving other people's bookings are both acts only the person working the day can weigh. The test is one question: can overtime hold every session today? It is a test, not a command. The day owner may clear the day even where a push would fit, because the ruling is theirs. Drawn for the Clinician; a Practitioner rules their own Day Sheet the same way inside their own Client organization.
Rule
Critical is the service's property, not the person's. Another injury on the sheet does not move. A screening moves. The rule lives on the service so the same sheet reads the same way whoever owns it.
Rule
A moved Order re-enters the slot search the way any Order does and is never dropped, which is Process 04's standing rule. Every moved Patient and their Employer contact are told at the moment of the move. Site, date and time cross the wall, nothing else.
Rule
Overtime is recorded time, nothing more. Process 09 bills time, so a day that runs long is billed as it ran, with no special object and no approval step inside this process.
Open
Whether an Order raised by a case in Process 06 or by the clock in Process 08 may also be urgent. Today only the Employer marks it. Not ruled.
In IMS
The complaint and injury reference records the gap directly: no triage step exists, and the workflow begins at the clinical encounter with no disposition before it. Nothing carries a priority on an order, so the flag, the Day Sheet alert and both absorb moves are built new.
Roles
Clinicians, Practitioners, Employers

L2 05a-CN: Confirm the consents

Sits under Process 05a, gate 2: Consent

Question it answers
How nine separate consents behave as one gate, and what happens when any single one is missing.
Answer
The required set is computed from the service, every one of them must be satisfied, and any single refusal ends the Service Order. There is no partial admission.
Rule
Consent is recorded against the person and the Client organization, not against the visit or the Service Order. That is what stops the Patient being asked the same thing on every visit. Later visits under that Client check what is held. They do not ask again.
Rule
The required set varies by service, the stored set does not. A pre-employment screen may require drug and alcohol consent where a complaint does not. So a person who has consented before can still have something outstanding, and it is only ever the difference that is asked for, never the whole set again.
Rule
Each consent is held against the version of the wording that was signed. When the wording changes, what was held against the old version is no longer current, and that one alone is asked again.
Rule
Refusing is a legitimate recorded outcome, not an error. The service does not proceed, and the Employer is told it was not completed, with no reason attached and no indication of which gate stopped it.
The nine
1. Take part in the assessment. 2. Collect and hold personal and health information. 3. Release the outcome to the Employer. 4. Contact the Patient's own treating doctor. 5. Drug and alcohol testing. 6. Receive survey invitations electronically. 7. Use de-identified data to improve the service. 8. Use data in research. 9. Informed consent to the procedure, which is taken in 05b because it is consent to an act rather than to holding a record.
In IMS
Storage exists for most of the set and the computation does not. `PatientConsent` holds it against an entity with a given date and a separate confirm date, which is the once-then-check pattern this process depends on. `ConsentSignature` carries privacy policy, electronic survey, research and drug and alcohol as separate grants, with a version and the signed file. `ConsentVerbal` is the procedure consent, and `PatientSurveyConsent` the survey one. `PESPatientDeclined` records a refusal. Six of the nine have a home. What is absent is the front of the process: nothing computes which consents a given service requires, so nothing can work out what is outstanding.
Roles
Patients, Clinicians, Employers

L2 05a-DR: Admit at the door

Sits under Process 05a, the door: locate the Order, confirm identity, hold the record

Question it answers
How the door gets from a person standing in front of it to a record it may write into, when the Order may be missing or wrong, the identification may be absent or stale, and the person may already exist in the system once, several times, or not at all.
Answer
Four checks in a fixed order, and only one of them ends the visit. Every defect in the Order has the same repair, the Employer, and most are fixed while the person waits. Identity is sighted, never asserted. The record is found within this Client or created, never joined across Clients. Only a wrong person stops the service outright.
Rule
Every decision at the door is the Clinician's, which is why every diamond sits in the Clinician's lane. The Patient attends, presents and answers, and decides nothing here. The Employer repairs the one record that is theirs, the Order.
Rule
The lookup has two speeds. The check-in code pulls up the Order and everything tied to it with certainty and no search. Without the code, the door searches by work email or employee number within this Client, which can mis-hit, and that risk is why the code exists.
Rule
Everything wrong with the Order has one repair. No Order in the system: the named Employer contact raises one from wherever they are, the Order issues its code, the contact texts it, and the person re-enters minutes later. Wrong Site or Department: amended the same way. A name gone stale after a marriage or a change of name: amended the same way, because the name on the Order is the Employer's data, not the door's. Work Healthy Australia never edits an Order, and an Order the Employer never raises means nobody is served and nothing is recorded anywhere.
Rule
No identification is not a stop, it is a re-book. The Order stays open and Process 04's no-show machinery books the return visit. Nothing about the attempt touches a Patient record, because no identity was confirmed against one.
Rule
A wrong person is the only hard stop. The service ends, nothing is opened, read or amended, and the Employer hears service not completed with no reason attached. Every other failure at the door is repairable while the person waits.
Rule
The duplicate search runs within this Client organization only, on the name and date of birth as they appear on the identification in hand, so the identity check and the duplicate check are one act on one document. One exact match, confirmed with the person, reuses the record. Nothing found creates one. Anything in between, a near name, the same birth date under a different spelling, is the Clinician's ruling in the room, never a merge the system performs on its own.
Rule
No search ever crosses Client organizations. Nothing could be reused from a cross-Client match anyway, since history and consent are captured fresh per Client, and the search itself would be a use of another Client's records. The same person under three Clients is three records, and none of the three knows the others exist.
Open
Whether the door re-sights identification on every visit or only the first under a Client. Sighting every visit is the strict reading of Gate 1. A lighter repeat-visit check, the code plus date of birth, is what most clinics practise. Not ruled.
In IMS
The gate does not exist. The fit-gap records no identity gate object and no analogue for the correct-Patient check or the stop-without-trace. `PESCandidateOnboardCode` is the ancestor of the check-in code, a one-time code issued before attendance, tied to onboarding rather than to admission. Nothing performs a duplicate search at the door, so the gate, the search and the repair loop are all built new.
Roles
Patients, Clinicians, Employers

L2 07-SU: Release without identifying anyone

Sits under Process 07, decision: Release without identifying anyone?

Question it answers
What the check actually is, given that a small enough group identifies its members by arithmetic.
Answer
Two passes, not one. Group size first, then whether any cell can be read from the others by subtraction.
Rule
The first pass is a count against a threshold, per reporting group. A group below it is merged upward into a larger one, or withheld.
Rule
The second pass is the one that gets forgotten. Where a breakdown is published alongside a total, a suppressed cell can be recovered by subtracting the others from it. Suppressing one cell means suppressing its complement too.
Rule
Reporting groups are defined before the survey runs, not after it. Discovering an under-threshold group at reporting time means the responses were collected and cannot be used.
Rule
This process never releases anything per person, so there is no consent gate on it. The release gate is arithmetic, and it is the only thing standing between a group finding and a privacy breach.
In IMS
Nothing. `Survey`, `SurveyCompany` and `SurveyGizmoResponse` hold responses, and no table anywhere carries a group size threshold, a suppression flag or a reporting-group definition. This subprocess is greenfield end to end, which is consistent with the survey capability itself being named after a product that no longer exists.
Roles
Clinicians, Practitioners

L2 08-DU: Compute what is due

Sits under Process 08, step: Compute what is due

Question it answers
Where the population comes from, given there is no worker roster, and how a due date is computed when more than one source sets an interval.
Answer
The population is people already seen, not people employed. The interval is the shorter of the test's own validity and whatever the Site or Role requires, added to the anchor date.
Rule
There is no roster, because a person is never onboarded as an employee. So this process cannot ask who works here. It walks two lists instead: dates that other processes set on records that already exist, and people who have already been seen under a Site or Role that carries an interval.
Rule
The consequence is worth stating plainly. Somebody never seen is never due, and Work Healthy Australia cannot know they exist. The first service creates the record the clock then watches.
Rule
The anchor is the baseline where there is one, and the last completed date otherwise. Not the date the last one was due, or a missed cycle silently shortens every cycle after it.
Rule
Due and overdue are named states with a band between them, not a single boundary. Due soon is what makes booking possible before the date passes.
Rule
It raises the Service Order and stops. Process 04 schedules it and Process 05 performs it.
In IMS
The raw materials and none of the machinery. Dates other processes set do exist: `CertOfCapacity.NextApptDate`, `ProviderRegistration.ExpiryDate`, and the next-treatment dates on the complaint chain. Test validity is `MedTest.TimeLimit`. Everything this subprocess actually does is absent: no anchor, no interval arithmetic, no due or overdue state, and nothing that walks a population. Confirmed by counting: one `DueDate` column across 513 tables, on a personal reminder.
Roles
Admin

L2 09-DB: Complete the site visit debrief

Sits under Process 09, step: Complete the site visit debrief

Question it answers
How one day at a Site becomes the billable record, now that billing is time entry and not a charge per service.
Answer
The day splits into four minute categories, the on-site minutes are allocated across the services actually delivered, and what the agreement covers is marked.
Rule
Four categories, and they are not interchangeable: travel, on site, debrief, and internal. Only some of them are billable under any given agreement, which is why the split has to happen before the marking.
Rule
The on-site minutes are allocated across the contracted services delivered, so a service rendered with no minutes against it is an error rather than a free service.
Rule
Lost time is recorded by category rather than left as a gap. That is what turns an unfilled hour from an unexplained hole into a measured one, and it answers the question of whether unfilled clinician time is billable without needing a separate rule.
Rule
The debrief is the time entry. It is not a note, and Process 09's invoice is built from it once the day is locked.
In IMS
Near-total parity, and the closest match in the whole set. `IMSShift` splits the day into travel, on site, debrief, internal and billing minutes exactly as drawn, and carries `BillToClientAgreement` for what the agreement covers and `Locked` with its user and date for the close. `IMSShiftService` allocates delivery minutes across the contracted services, with `IMSShiftServiceInternal` for the internal ones. Lost time is `IMSShiftException` with a category and its minutes. `tblIMSDebriefTime` and `ProviderActualMinutes` hold the raw time. Only the completeness check has no home.
Roles
Clinicians, Practitioners
