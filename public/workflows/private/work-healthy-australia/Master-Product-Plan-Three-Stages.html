Master product plan: three stages from pilot to industry practice

Written 2 August 2026 by the operator Chau, Edge8. Revised the same day against version 4, after the fit and gap against IMS closed. Implements the version 4 process set. Companion documents: Fit-Gap-New-Design-Against-IMS.md, HANDOFF-process-set-v3.md, L1-Gap-Check-Against-Industry.md, Spine-Programme-MVP-Delivery-Plan.md, the published diagrams at /artifacts.

1. What this document is

A plan to implement the version 4 process set as working software, in three stages, on a database designed once for all three. Version 4 is nine processes on eleven pages, 05 having grown a third page, plus Treatment as a predefined external box that is called and tracked but not defined. It builds on the new record, not the existing OccuSpan codebase, per the decision of 30 July.

The evidence base changed on 2 August. The fit and gap was run against full schema dumps of all three production databases: IMS v1 at 513 tables, IMS v2 at 316, CPORTAL at 39. The three questions the earlier handoff left for the client application analyst are answered. IMS raises no work from a date, so process 08 is confirmed greenfield. IMS does hold expiry-driven records that persist across visits, so process 06 is much closer to parity than assumed. And task analysis happens in IMS, in the tblJobs cluster, which settles what process 03 migrates from. The dumps are schema only, so they establish what IMS can hold, not how much of it is used; row counts remain the outstanding evidence.

Two words are used precisely throughout, as in the programme plan. Verified means a process ran and produced a record. Proposed marks a judgement of mine that nobody has yet agreed.

The three stages, named for what each one proves:

StageNameWhat it proves
1Prove the serviceOne pilot client runs pre-employment screening and complaint or injury evaluation end to end on the new record, through review and release, witnessed. The migration pipeline is proven feasible on a selected slice: obfuscate, migrate, unobfuscate, verify
2Carry the estateEverything IMS, the Bubble Task Analyzer and the scheduling tool do today runs on the new record, including case management and treatment tracking at IMS parity, with the history migrated and reconciled
3Run the programmeThe clock is live and everything upgrades to it: check-ups raised from dates, cases driven by certificate expiry instead of booked next visits, and workplace risk assessed at the Site and group level

The stages gate features, never schema. Every table for all three stages is created in the first migration set. A stage turns capabilities on by adding rows to the capability matrix and flipping module flags, which are data. This is what makes the no-rewire requirement achievable: the schema never learns anything new, it only starts being read.

This plan builds on three version 4 choices that still carry the operator's sign-off flag in the fit and gap: 05b drawn for the Clinician with the Practitioner as a rule, 03 rating task risk where it measures the demand, and the 05c reviewer defined by registration rather than by role. If any of the three is reversed, the affected sections are 2, 3 and 7.

2. The three stages in full

Stage 1: Prove the service

Built out in detail in MVP-Implementation-Plan.md, with MVP-Functional-Architecture.html and MVP-Technical-Architecture.html. That document carries the steps, the engines and the verification harness; this section carries the scope and the exit.

One pilot client. The core processes exactly as drawn in version 4: 01 Setup Workplace, 02 Onboard / Offboard, 03 Define the Role Requirements, 04 Schedule the Service, 05a admission, 05b render and determine, 05c review and release, and 09 Report to Invoice. That set is sufficient to render and prove the two services named for the pilot: pre-employment screening, and complaint or injury evaluation. Both are 05 services; they differ in trigger and purpose, not in shape.

Only the necessary setups, which are:

  1. The pilot client's hierarchy: Client organization, Sites, Departments where they exist, Roles, loaded complete before any Service Order can exist. Parents above the Client organization exist in the schema and are not needed for a single-client pilot.
  2. The contracted services and their term, and the named client contacts with what each receives, because 09 compiles delivered services against the contracted set and the report needs an addressee.
  3. Accounts and assignments for the Clinicians serving the pilot, and any Practitioners the pilot client contracts, through 02, including the grant of what each person may perform, derived from their confirmed credentials. Data scope and function scope are both live from the first account.
  4. Signed Role requirements documents for every Role in pilot scope, produced through 03 by measurement on site, with the evidence attached and the demands recorded on the same scale the functional tests report. These are authored fresh in the new system, not migrated. Nothing is determined against a draft.
  5. The Site Service calendar with the working days for the year, the hours within them, and assigned providers, so 04 can book. Paperwork goes out before the day, so the door verifies consent rather than collects it, and the work email check on the Service Order establishes that the Patient belongs to the client.
  6. The employer wall, live from the first day. Every Employer-facing output in the pilot is a release document, a separate object, never a filtered view of the clinical record. The pilot delivers determinations to a real Employer, so the wall cannot wait for a later stage.
  7. The 05c gate. Where the service requires review before release, the reviewer holds the registration the service calls for and the review is recorded with the reviewer's name. A clinically significant finding outside the referral question is told to the Patient and referred to their own doctor from the first pilot service, because a duty of care does not wait for a later stage.
  8. Report of services rendered and invoices through 09 under the time-entry model: the debrief is the time entry, split into travel, on site, debrief and internal minutes, the day is locked before it is invoiced, and the invoice is built from the locked minutes. There is no billable event.
  9. The migration rehearsal. The pilot proves the data migration procedure on a selected set of tables and rows before stage 2 depends on it: obfuscate, migrate, unobfuscate, verify. Real records are obfuscated at the source so that no identified health data passes through the transformation tooling, the obfuscated rows are migrated into the new tables under the same rules stage 2 will use, the values are restored on the target side, and the result is verified field for field against the source. With the schema dumps in hand the slice can be named now rather than at gate zero; section 5 proposes it.

What stage 1 deliberately does not include: bulk migration from IMS or Bubble beyond the rehearsal slice, Employer self-service beyond raising a Service Order and receiving outputs, and everything in processes 06, 07 and 08. A pilot complaint or injury evaluation ends at the released determination and its mitigation plan; the persistent case is stage 2.

Exit criteria. A real applicant completes a pre-employment screening and a real employee completes a complaint or injury evaluation, each driven end to end through a real sign-in as each role that touches it, witnessed by the Work Healthy Australia person who does that job today. Review before release happened where the service required it, with the reviewer recorded. The Employer received the determination as a release document and nothing else. The invoice went out from locked minutes and was paid. No step of the service path read a legacy system. And the migration rehearsal round-tripped its selected slice under the acceptance criteria in section 5.

Stage 2: Carry the estate

Everything the current estate does today moves onto the new record: IMS, the Bubble Task Analyzer, and the scheduling tool. The fit and gap replaced inference with a table-by-table reading of what IMS can hold, so this stage's boundary is now drawn from evidence, with row counts still to confirm which capabilities are alive.

  1. Task analysis history migrates, from IMS first. The tblJobs cluster is a complete job demands record: minimum ratings on the same scales the functional tests report, the ManTRA assessment across five body regions, postural and manual handling demands, images, video and the employer's own procedures. It lands in the 03 tables as versioned Role requirements documents with evidence attached. The Employer sign-off and the review clock are new by design; migrated documents enter as drafts awaiting sign-off, because IMS has no signature to carry. The Bubble application holds six years of the same work; whether it migrates as a second source or is archived and decommissioned is decided by row counts, and the reconciliation between the two is part of this stage.
  2. Case management and treatment tracking reach IMS parity. This is the largest change from the earlier read. IMS holds the case, the certificate of capacity, the duties plan with review reasons, barriers to recovery, advice split by audience, and treatment across many visits, which is its largest data volume. All of it lands in the 06 tables and runs in stage 2 the way IMS runs it today: the next visit is booked directly through 04, because there is no clock yet. The claim reference rides on the case. Treatment is referred, attendance and progress are tracked, and the episode itself stays external, as ruled.
  3. Scheduling reaches parity with the estate and the industry pattern. Employer-raised requests with an accept or reject decision and a recorded reason, including whether the request falls inside the contracted working days. Employer amendments to bookings they hold. Confirmation to both the named Employer contact and the Patient at booking. Travel time in the slot search. Estimated periods resolved from the client and the service together. The no-show report feeding follow-up.
  4. The commercial half runs on time entry at full breadth. Delivery minutes per contracted service as the evidence under the locked day, the report comparing delivered against contracted minutes, and late cancellation and failure to attend recorded but never billed, because the time was reserved and consumed.
  5. The standing clinical record fills. Complaint and medical history migrate and become the standing medical history that 05b reviews and updates at every visit. Consents migrate with their versions, so held consent that predates the current wording is asked again rather than assumed.
  6. Every client, every site. Stage 1's single-client scoping rules are unchanged; the population grows to the full register, including organization parents above the Client organization where groups exist.

Exit criteria. The capability register shows no In MVP capability unmet. The parity gate is signed: every migrated capability demonstrated against the same scenarios the service handles today, witnessed by the Work Healthy Australia user who does that job. The transfer gate has run: one real client runs a full screening, a complaint or injury evaluation and a case review on the new record with the current system in read-only parallel and the outputs reconciled. IMS and Bubble go read-only, restorable for a defined period, not switched off on the day.

Stage 3: Run the programme

The clock, and everything that upgrades once it exists. Process 08 is confirmed greenfield: across all 513 tables of IMS v1 there is one due-date column, and it is a clinician's personal reminder. But its inputs are half parity, because IMS already puts requirements on the Company Role and gives each test a validity period. Stage 3 builds the computation and the watch.

  1. 08 Track Fitness Check-ups. The one process that watches a calendar. Requirements live on the Site and the Role, never on the person, because there is no worker roster by design; the Role arrives on the Service Order from the Employer who nominated the person. Two intervals are read, the test's own validity and the Site or Role requirement, and the shorter wins. Due items raise Service Orders without an Employer asking, and a due and overdue position crosses the wall as dates and counts. This is what turns individually requested services into a monitoring programme that can be sold.
  2. 06 upgrades from booked visits to expiry-driven. Stage 2 runs cases the way IMS does, by booking the next appointment. Stage 3 replaces the workaround with the design: the certificate carries an expiry, 08 watches it, and the case sets dates and stops. The diagrams already draw this; the stage flips which mechanic is live.
  3. 07 Assess and Control Workplace Risk. Surveys first, as ruled. Task-level risk ratings already exist from stage 2, because 03 rates each task where it measures the demand; 07 works above the single task, at the Site, Department and group level. Hazards carry ratings, not a yes or no. Controls are the Employer's to implement and the recording of what was implemented is ours. The incident record, written at the Site when the incident happens, becomes a trigger. Findings go back to the people who answered before action planning, and the review date carries the re-survey.

The build order inside stage 3 is 08 first, because 06 and 07 both end by setting a date and are incomplete without something to hand it to. By the time 08 turns on, its due-item table already holds every date stage 1 and 2 wrote: review dates on temporary determinations, credential expiries, monitoring intervals from Role requirements documents, and certificate expiries from stage 2 cases. The clock starts with history, not empty.

Exit criteria. A worker whose check-up interval has lapsed appears as overdue without anyone building a list, and the Service Order the clock raises flows through 04 and 05 unchanged. A case runs on certificate expiry with no manually booked next visit. A risk assessment publishes findings that identify nobody and produces a control plan whose review date returns as the next assessment.

3. Supabase database scaffolding

All tables exist from the first migration set. The design rules below are what make the no-rewire requirement hold; each absorbs a known future change so the change arrives as rows, not as ALTER TABLE.

Design rules

  1. One spine object. service_orders is the request object for every service in all three stages, including 07's group work. Its subject is either a person record or a place in the hierarchy: person_record_id is nullable, and a check constraint requires exactly one of person or site-level subject. The Order carries the nominated Role from the Employer, the work-email entitlement check, and the rejection fields for a request that cannot be met, including whether it fell inside the contracted working days. Purpose is an enumerated column covering all stages from day one.
  2. Every date flows to one table. Any process that decides a date writes a row to due_items: review dates on temporary determinations, credential expiries, monitoring intervals, certificate expiries, control plan reviews. Stage 1 and 2 write it; nothing reads it until stage 3 turns the clock on. Interval resolution honours the version 4 rule: a test's own validity and a Site or Role interval are both read, and the shorter wins.
  3. The wall is a schema, not a filter. Everything an Employer may see lives in the wall schema: release documents, audience classes, the release log, group scores, and the due and overdue views. Employer-facing surfaces are granted access to the wall schema and nothing else, so "no employer response contains a clinical field" is enforceable by grant and answerable by a query, not by reading application code.
  4. Authorization is data, on two axes. Data scope is the five roles and the capability matrix, deny by default, one guard, row-level security policies joining the matrix. Function scope is a grant table fed by credentials: which services a person may perform, which they may review, who may unlock a locked day. Two Clinicians are not interchangeable, and 04 checks the grant before assigning a slot. Every grant and revocation is dated, and access is archived, never deleted, so a past action is attributable to the scope in force when it happened.
  5. One person record per person per client organization. Add, never move. Enforced by a unique constraint and a trigger that rejects any update to the organization column. The applicant and the employee are two records by design, and no worker roster exists: the Employer nominates who receives a service, per Service Order. Every record is created from its visible parent and is born knowing client, site, department, role, person and purpose, none of which can change afterwards.
  6. Nothing is deleted. Sites, Departments, Roles, assignments and grants are end-dated, so every historical record stays resolvable and closed things drop out of the pickers.
  7. Billing is time entry. There is no billable-event table, because there is no billable event. The debrief is the time entry, minutes are categorised, the day locks before invoicing, and the invoice traces to locked minutes with delivered services as evidence.
  8. Migration is anticipated in the shape. Every table that can receive migrated data carries three lineage columns: legacy_system, legacy_table, legacy_id. Migrated clinical scores additionally carry a provenance value, trusted or recomputed, because the recompute-versus-trust ruling must be visible on every number it governs, and IMS's stored ManTRA scores now sit under that ruling alongside Bubble's.
  9. Row-level security on every table from the first migration, deny by default, failing closed with a plain sentence. No table is created without its policies.

The table inventory

Schemas separate the concerns that must never leak into each other. Views and reports (the day sheet, the weekly no-show report, delivered against contracted, task fitness summaries) are derived and are not listed; only stored objects are. Everything is active from stage 1 unless the row says otherwise.

4. Adoptability and verifiability

These decide whether the product is accepted at all, and they are the specific risk of a product produced with Claude. AI-produced work has been rejected in this account before, and the failure was never the output: it was that nobody witnessed the output run against a yardstick they trusted. The design answer is that nobody is ever asked to trust the author. They are asked to watch the evidence.

The process maps are the acceptance criteria. Every capability demonstrates against its published version 4 diagram, which the operator reviewed one process at a time and which carries the rules Work Healthy Australia agreed. A demonstration is a walk down the swimlane: this step, this gate, this output crossing the wall and nothing else. The yardstick was agreed before the software existed, so passing it is not self-certification. And the yardstick itself has now been checked table by table against what IMS actually holds, so "the diagram is right" and "IMS does it differently" are no longer the same argument.

Every pass is role-stamped. A capability lands with the flow driven end to end through a real sign-in as each role that touches it: the Clinician, the Practitioner, the Employer viewer, the Admin. No authentication bypass, one stored record per pass. Nothing in the current product was ever verified as a non-administrator, which is precisely why its authorization cannot be trusted; the replacement makes the opposite claim provable from the first capability. Function scope is verified the same way: a Clinician without the audiometry grant cannot be assigned an audiometry Order, and the refusal is on screen.

The witness is the person who does the job. The parity gate is not a test suite passing; it is the Work Healthy Australia user who runs screenings today watching the new record run one, and signing. The transfer gate is one real client's work on the new record with the old system reconciled beside it. Adoption is built by making the people who hold the knowledge the judges of the replacement, which is the same reason the programme is named for what it builds.

Failure speaks plainly. Every closed gate produces a plain sentence: which credential expired, why the hierarchy blocks the request, that the person attending is not the Patient on the Order, that a request falls outside the contracted working days. Clinicians adopt systems that tell them what is wrong in their own nouns, and every noun in this system is one they already use.

Authorization is answerable by a query. Who can read a clinical note is one row lookup, not 336 files. Who may perform or review a service is one row lookup in the grants. The wall is a schema grant. The audit trail is append-only and usable as evidence in both directions: it proves the wall held, and it is what a plaintiff subpoenas if it did not. A verifier does not have to read the code Claude wrote; they query the structures it runs on.

No identified health record passes through the tooling. The migration pipeline is obfuscate, migrate, unobfuscate, verify, rehearsed in stage 1 and scaled in stage 2. The transformation tooling, including any AI assisting it, works on structurally faithful, personally meaningless data, and the real values are restored only on the target side under the source custodian's control. The question a privacy owner will ask about an AI-assisted migration is answered by the pipeline's shape, not by an assurance.

Quality before scale. Stage 1 is deliberately one client and two services, and it must be excellent before stage 2 multiplies it across the full client base and six years of history. Nothing scales until the small version has been witnessed working. The pilot is also the enablement vehicle: Work Healthy Australia engineers pair on it, and each stage produces a runbook for the person who owns it after handover.

Two names are still blocking. The wall is live in stage 1, so the privacy owner must be named before the pilot goes live, not before a later stage. Clinical governance must be named before any migrated score lands, because recompute-versus-trust is their ruling, and it now covers IMS's stored ManTRA scores as well as Bubble's instruments. Both must be Work Healthy Australia people. This plan does not soften the programme plan's position on either.

5. Data migration: absolute acceptance criteria

Migration acceptance is stated per stage as criteria that either hold or do not. No partial passes. The sources are now named: IMS v1 at 513 tables, IMS v2 at 316 with back-references to v1, CPORTAL at 39, and the Bubble application, reverse-engineered separately. The dumps are schema only, so a row-count pass over the named tables precedes any mapping work; a table with no rows is an abandoned capability whatever the schema suggests.

Stage 1 accepts on setup completeness and a proven migration rehearsal

Stage 1 loads setup data fresh and migrates nothing in bulk. Its first criteria are about completeness of setup:

  1. The pilot client hierarchy is complete, and an incomplete hierarchy blocks Service Order creation with a plain sentence.
  2. The pilot client's contracted services, term and named contacts are recorded, because 09 cannot compile without them.
  3. Every Role in pilot scope has a signed, active Role requirements document with its evidence attached. Nothing was determined against a draft.
  4. No step of either pilot service reads IMS, Bubble, CPORTAL or any legacy system. The pilot proves the new record stands alone.

Alongside the services, stage 1 pilots the migration procedure itself, on a selected set of tables and rows, so that stage 2 scales a pipeline that has already worked rather than attempting one for the first time at full volume. The pipeline is obfuscate, migrate, unobfuscate, verify, and it accepts only when all of the following hold:

  1. The slice is named before it moves. Which tables, which rows, and why those, recorded with a name against the choice. The slice must exercise the known hazards, not avoid them. With the schema in hand the candidate slice can be proposed now: the tblJobs cluster, because it carries stored computed ManTRA scores under the recompute-versus-trust ruling; CertOfCapacity with TreatmentDuties, because their state reconstructs from flags and dates; and ConsentSignature, because it is versioned and its currency must survive the move. Archive-flagged rows are included deliberately, because tombstone filtering is one of the things being proven.
  2. Obfuscation holds at the boundary. Every direct identifier and every clinical value in the slice is obfuscated at the source, before the data reaches the transformation tooling. No identified health record passes through the middle of the pipeline. What the tooling sees is structurally faithful and personally meaningless.
  3. The migration step runs under stage 2's rules. Obfuscated rows land in the section 3 tables with lineage populated, exactly one status per record, and the same mapping logic stage 2 will reuse. The rehearsal proves the rules, not just the plumbing.
  4. Unobfuscation is exact. Restoring the original values on the target side is lossless. The obfuscation map never leaves the source custodian's control, and it is destroyed or retained by their ruling, recorded.
  5. Verification is field for field. Every migrated row in the slice compares equal to its source across every mapped field after unobfuscation, or carries a signed variance. Row counts on the slice reconcile exactly. The comparison itself is stored as evidence in migration_reconciliations.
  6. The pipeline is repeatable by someone else. The rehearsal produces a runbook, and the acceptance is that a second run of the same slice by a different operator produces the same result.

Stage 2 accepts only against reconciled history

Stage 2 runs the pipeline stage 1 proved, at full scale: the same obfuscate, migrate, unobfuscate, verify sequence, the same runbook, the same evidence table. Its own criteria are additional, because volume introduces failure modes a slice cannot:

  1. Row counts precede mapping. Every table named in the fit and gap gets a live row count before its migration is scoped. Counts decide which Covered capabilities are actually in use, which of tblJobs and the Bubble application is the richer task analysis source, and whether the STCApproved release gate was a working control or a dead field.
  2. Snapshot before anything moves. A faithful, as-is copy of every source system is taken before any transformation, and a restore of that snapshot has been tested. A snapshot is not a migration; it is the rollback floor and the audit floor.
  3. Every migrated row carries lineage. Legacy system, legacy table, legacy identifier, populated on every row that came from the estate. A record that cannot say where it came from is not accepted.
  4. Row counts reconcile, tombstone-filtered. Per migrated table: source count after archive-flag filtering equals target count plus a signed variance ledger naming every excluded row class and who accepted the exclusion.
  5. Recompute versus trust is ruled before the first score lands. Clinical governance decides whether stored computed values migrate as they are or are recomputed by ported scoring engines. Every migrated score carries its provenance. The ruling covers IMS's ManTRA scores on tblJobs and Bubble's instruments alike; the confirmed REBA defect in Bubble means at least one instrument cannot be trusted as stored.
  6. State reconstructs to exactly one status. Source records hold state across archive flags, dates and booleans. The reconstruction rules are written down, and a sample of reconstructed records per record type is witnessed by the person who works those records today.
  7. Migrated Role requirements enter as drafts. IMS's tblJobs has no signature, so no migrated document lands as active. Each is signed through 03's own sign-off step before anything is determined against it. Parity for the data, the new rule for the yardstick.
  8. Open work moves without loss of state. Open Service Orders, bookings, in-progress assessments, unexpired certificates and open cases are the only records that must move live; closed history may follow. An open item that cannot be placed in a target state stops the migration rather than landing as something it is not.
  9. Migration order is dependency order. Hierarchy and people, then Role requirements documents, then historical assessments and histories, then open work, then closed history.
  10. The parallel run reconciles. Same client, same week, both systems, outputs compared, every discrepancy dispositioned with a name.
  11. The parity and transfer gates are signed, per section 2. Migration is not accepted by the migrating engineer; it is accepted by the register and the witnesses.

Stage 3 accepts against the harvest

Stage 3 migrates no bulk data. It harvests the dates already in the record into the clock:

  1. Every date-carrying record in the migrated and stage-1-native data produces exactly one due item, or a recorded waiver with a reason and an approver. The count of date-carrying records equals the count of due items plus waivers, provable by query.
  2. Certificates migrated in stage 2 with a booked next visit convert cleanly: the expiry drives the due item, and no case carries both mechanics at once.
  3. A person known to be overdue, seeded as a test against a real requirement, appears on the due and overdue position without anyone building a list.
  4. No due item exists without a resolvable source record.

6. Data retention outside these processes

The estate holds 868 tables across the three dumps: 513 in IMS v1, 316 in IMS v2, 39 in CPORTAL, before counting the Bubble application. The nine processes read nothing like that many, and migrating what no process reads would be inference dressed as diligence. The policy is broad by design; the specifics belong to the row-count pass.

Every table gets exactly one disposition, decided from row counts first, because a table with no live rows is an abandoned capability whatever its screen suggests. The four dispositions:

  1. Migrate. The table feeds an object in section 3. Semantic migration, one domain at a time, under the stage 2 criteria.
  2. Archive. Live data that no process in this plan reads: it moves to the legacy schema or equivalent cold storage as a faithful extract, read-only, platform administrator only, reachable from a live person record through the lineage columns. Archived is findable; it is not modelled.
  3. Tombstone. Soft-deleted and abandoned content. It exists in the snapshot and nowhere else.
  4. Retire. Capability deliberately ended, with a named decision-maker, per the register. Its data is snapshot-only unless a retention clock says otherwise.

Two dispositions are already signalled by the fit and gap. The practices recommended against carrying forward, such as the preferred gender and rate of pay columns on the job demands record and the plain credential columns, are data that archives but never migrates. And drug and alcohol results held in IMS need a ruling of their own: Work Healthy Australia does no sampling, but whether a third party's result may be recorded is a different question from collecting one, and the answer decides whether those tables archive or migrate into the clinical record.

The statutory clocks outlive both systems. Health monitoring records carry thirty-year retention for hazardous chemicals and lead and forty for asbestos, and the employer's copy has its own clock and its own owner. Whatever the disposition of a table, any record class under a statutory clock must remain producible as a durable document for the full period. That is a property of the archive, not of the live system, and it is why the snapshot and the legacy archive are permanent fixtures rather than migration scaffolding.

IMS stays restorable after cutover for a defined period, readable throughout the parallel run and rollback window. Switching a source system off is a decision with a date, taken on gate evidence, never a side effect of the migration finishing.

Disposal is a recorded event. When a retention clock does expire, the disposal writes a disposal_events row naming what was disposed, under which clock, by whose authority. Automated disposal is deferred; the clocks and the register are not.

8. What this plan does not claim

It does not claim durations; those belong to the people doing the work. It does not claim row counts: the schema dumps prove what IMS can hold, not what it holds, and the row-count pass is the first act of stage 2. It does not resolve the open items that survived version 4, consent scope per service type, the health check result, credential expiries in 08, and the anonymous survey's consequence; it shows where each lands so that resolving them later costs rows, not rewiring. It does not decide the Bubble application's disposition; row counts against tblJobs do. And it builds on the three version 4 choices flagged for the operator's sign-off in the fit and gap; if any is reversed, sections 2, 3 and 7 revise.
