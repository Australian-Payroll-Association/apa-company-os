Task Boards

Why

Work happens on three fronts, and none of it is tracked in one place

Edge8 runs three kinds of work at once, and today each lives in a different head or thread:

  • Client projects. Delivery for Australian Payroll, Work Healthy, Arca Wellness, and the rest: the tasks that move a client roadmap forward, with no shared view of what is in flight or how close it is to done.
  • Our own products. Building AIOlabz, running Eight Edges: sprint work that needs a backlog, an owner per task, and a way to see the week.
  • Day to day. Operations that just has to get done (lease renewals, equipment intake, vendor cleanup, payroll runs) plus the promises made in 1-1s, which today only surface at the next 1-1.

One board model covers all three. A board can belong to a client, to a product, or to a function, and the work shows up the same way: who owns it, what column it is in, how long it has sat there. Commitments from coaching and issues from the Eight Edges flow in as cards rather than living in their own silos.

And because Edge8 already has agents filing issues when numbers slip, the same pattern extends here: agents are board users, filing cards from scheduled routines and moving them when the work completes. That is the difference between this and Trello.

The one-sentence pitch: one place that answers "who is doing what, and how long has it been sitting there", across every client, product, and function, for humans and agents alike.

Concept model

Boards, columns, cards, and one link type

Board
Name, slug, owner, and an optional linked client. Any number of them.
Column
Per-board, ordered, renamable. Not a fixed enum. One column is flagged "done".
Card
A row in tasks: assignee (name shown on the card), priority, due date, position in a column, optional sprint.
Sprint
Per-board, optional: a named date range with a goal. No sprint = backlog or ongoing work.
Link
subject_type / subject_id points a card at a commitment, an item on the board's client roadmap, or nothing.

The commitment tie-in

Commitments link, they never duplicate. From the coaching view, "Push to board" creates a card with subject_type = coaching_commitment. The commitment stays the promise (coaching context, owned by the coach); the card is the work record (assignee, column, due date). Moving the card into the board's done column marks the commitment kept. The sync is one-way, card to commitment, so the coaching flow never breaks if a board changes.

The client and roadmap tie-in

A board optionally links to a client company. That link does two things. First, it scopes roadmaps: on a client board, a card can link to an item on that client's roadmap (the client_backlog_items behind Eight Edges client roadmaps), with the picker showing only that client's items. The card gets a ROADMAP badge and the roadmap view shows which items have live cards. Second, it powers the client view: the linked client sees their board in the portal. One link slot, one meaning: a card is about one thing, a commitment or a roadmap item, never both.

Sprints

Sprints are per-board and optional. A sprint is a named date range with an optional goal; a card can belong to at most one. AIOlabz can run two-week sprints while Operations flows continuously and never touches them. Sprint is time, columns are workflow state, so there is never a "Sprint 34" column. The board toolbar carries a sprint picker (active sprint by default, plus backlog and all); closing a sprint explicitly rolls unfinished cards to the next one, and every rollover is logged.

Rules that keep boards honest

  • Every column move is logged (task_stage_log), so every card shows its age in the current column. Older than 7 days shows an amber clock.
  • Cards archive (timestamp), never delete. The app role has no DELETE grant, and history is the point.
  • An agent-filed card is labeled AGENT and carries its source routine in metadata, so "why does this card exist" always has an answer.

Board membership

Team members see only boards they belong to. Membership is explicit (managed in board settings) and automatic on assignment: giving someone a card adds them to that board. Admins see everything. Clients see exactly one thing: the board linked to them.

Decision needed: every card on a client board is client-visible unless marked internal on the card. If you'd rather default the other way (internal unless marked client-visible), say so before PR 1; it is one flag either way.

Agents on the board

Cards filed by routines, not just people

Scheduled routines already produce findings (fleet fitness grades, QBO sync diffs, issues when numbers slip). Phase 2 gives them a hand on the board:

  1. File: a routine creates a card in a board's intake column with priority, due date, and a metadata block naming the routine and its evidence.
  2. Move: when the underlying condition clears (the laptop is replaced, the invoice syncs), the routine moves its own card to done with a note.
  3. Nudge: a weekly sweep flags cards past their amber threshold in the standup briefing. No new channel, it rides the existing rhythm.

Scope

What phase 1 is not

  • No checklists inside cards, no attachments, no comments (subject links cover context; comments can come later if silence hurts).
  • No swimlanes, WIP limits, or automations builder. Client portal view is read-only: clients cannot add or move cards in phase 1.
  • No refactor of the five existing boards. They stay as they are; the shared component just stops the count at six.

Done when

Phase 1 success criteria

  1. An Operations board exists with real cards, and a card can be created, assigned, dragged between columns, and archived, with every move logged.
  2. A commitment made in a 1-1 can be pushed to a board in one click, and marking the card done marks the commitment kept.
  3. Every team member sees their member boards and one "My tasks" list on /team, and nothing else.
  4. Card age in column and assignee name are visible on every card without opening it.
  5. One board runs a real sprint: cards assigned to it, the picker filters to it, and closing it rolls the leftovers forward.
  6. On a client board, a card links to an item on that client's roadmap (badge on the card, live-card marker on the roadmap), and the client sees the board in their portal minus internal cards.
