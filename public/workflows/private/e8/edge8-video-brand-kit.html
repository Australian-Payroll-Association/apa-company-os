Brand rules, enforced on screen and in copy.

"Edge8" is never written in all caps. Not in titles, not in captions, not in descriptions, not in the thumbnail.

No em dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a colon, or a middle dot. This applies to burned-in captions, video titles, and post copy.

Why mint carries the episode number. Mint is the only saturated colour in the palette, and on a near-black field it is the first thing the eye lands on. In a feed, a viewer sees the thumbnail for well under a second, so the number gets the mint and everything else stays quiet. This is also the one place boldness is spent, the rest of the frame is deliberately plain.

The rail is the one non-obvious element, and it earns its place. This is a daily series, so most viewers arrive mid-run rather than at E00. The rail answers "where am I and how much is there" without a word of voiceover, and it makes the three arcs legible as a shape. If the episode count changes, the rail changes with it, so it stays honest.

Trade-off: it costs 5px of frame and one more thing for the eye to parse. If review finds it noisy, cut it. The card still works without it.

04 · Caption style

Built for a muted autoplay on a phone

LinkedIn autoplays muted, so captions are burned in, not a sidecar file. This is the asset most likely to fail in the field, because a dashboard recorded at 1080p and watched at 380px wide is unreadable if the caption competes with the UI.

RuleValueWhy
TypeManrope 600 · 52px · line-height 1.2452px at 1920 wide is roughly 10px on a 380px phone, which is the floor for comfortable reading

Line limitMax 2 lines · about 42 characters per lineThree lines cover the UI, which is the thing the episode exists to show
ChunkingBreak on clause, never mid-phraseCaptions are read in glances, so each line should stand alone
Safe area96px inset on all sides, 5%Nothing meaningful outside it, in case a platform crops
PunctuationNo em dashes. Comma, colon, or middle dotBrand rule, applies to burned-in text as much as to copy

The real risk here is not the caption, it is the UI behind it. Recording a dense dashboard at native zoom produces text nobody can read in a feed. Recommend the recording rig runs browser zoom at 140% with a deliberate zoom on each payoff frame, and that this gets settled on the pilot episode before any arc is batch recorded. Getting it wrong means re-recording six episodes, not one.

06 · Handoff

How this becomes files the pipeline can use

The rig assembles with ffmpeg, so it needs PNGs and a caption format, not a web page. This section is what a developer or an agent needs to generate the set.

  1. Approve the direction first. One episode reviewed and signed off is worth more than 17 exported cards that then get redone.
  2. Generate 17 title cards and 17 thumbnails by driving this page from a script, one screenshot per episode at the exact frame size. Same rig that records the episodes can render the cards.
  3. Export two end cards: the standard one, and the E16 variant that closes with "Talk to Edge8."
  4. Captions ship as ASS, not SRT. SRT carries no styling, so the 86% scrim and the position would be lost. The ffmpeg subtitles filter reads ASS styling directly and burns it in.
  5. Add the four brand checks to per-episode QC: no client data on screen, "Edge8" casing correct, no em dashes anywhere in captions or titles, voiceover and screen in sync at each beat.
