Teknorot Australia: the Ordering and Cash Dashboard

Warren runs a deliberately lean parts business on clean systems: Peach for inventory, Xero for accounting, Auto Info for orders. The data is all there. Nobody is reporting on it. This program turns the three questions he asked on the discovery call into three dashboard panels, built on a central database he owns, by him, at the Sydney retreat.

Why

Three questions, in Warren's own words

Every dashboard starts with a decision, not with data. Warren asked three questions on the call. Each one is a decision he cannot currently make, and each becomes one panel.

"How do you know how much money we'll have in February?" Cash flow forecasting against long stock lead times. He orders months ahead and cannot see whether the cash will be there when the stock lands.PANEL 1 · CASH POSITION FORECAST

"How many stock days do we have, what's our stock turn, how much stock do we need, when are our peaks?" Stock coverage and reorder timing. Ordering today is "just going and going", with no reorder points and no view of seasonality.PANEL 2 · STOCK COVERAGE

"We might be ordering our fastest moving stock but making the least margin on it. We should be ordering our medium stock and making more margin." Margin-weighted ordering. He suspects the mix is wrong and has no way to put a number on it.PANEL 3 · MARGIN MIX

The method

Decision first, data second, AI last

More than 70% of a dashboard build is not the dashboard. It is deciding what you are trying to decide, then getting the right data into a place you own. Once that foundation exists, the reports are conversations with Claude, not engineering projects.

1. Decision
What am I trying to decide? Warren already named all three.
2. Data
What does that decision need, and which system holds it today?
3. Workflow
How does the data move from Peach and Xero into the central database?
4. Build
AI builds the panel on top. Warren asks for changes in plain English.
The foundationA central Supabase database Warren owns, fed by exports from the systems he already runs. Not API calls scattered through a website builder: the data lands in his database first, and everything else, including the website his contractor is building, can sit on top of it. Start with manual CSV exports at the retreat; automate the pipelines after. Done when: one Peach export and one Xero export load cleanly, and Claude answers "what were my top 20 SKUs by revenue last quarter?" from his own data.

The panels

Three decisions, three panels

Panel 1

Cash Position Forecast

POST-RETREAT
DECISION IT SERVES

Can I pay for the stock that's arriving, and how much can I commit to the next order?

DATA

Xero bank balance, receivables and payables with due dates. Peach open purchase orders with ETAs and payment terms. Trailing sales run rate for the inflow line.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE

A forward cash line, month by month for six months, with stock payments overlaid as the big outflows. February is a point on the line, not a guess.

DONE WHEN

The projected month-end balance for each of the next six months is on screen, and it changes when Warren adds a hypothetical purchase order.

Panel 2

Stock Coverage

RETREAT DAY 2, IF TIME
DECISION IT SERVES

What do I reorder now, how much, and what can wait?

DATA

Peach stock on hand by SKU. Sales velocity on a rolling 90 days, plus the same period last year for peaks. Supplier lead times.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE

Stock days and stock turn per SKU, a reorder point per SKU (lead time demand plus buffer), and a ranked "order these next" list.

DONE WHEN

The list flags every SKU that will stock out inside its lead time, and nothing else.

Panel 3

Margin Mix

RETREAT DAY 2, FIRST BUILD
DECISION IT SERVES

Order for profit, not just for speed.

DATA

Peach sales lines with cost and sell price per SKU. One export, one table: which is exactly why it is the first build.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE

A quadrant of velocity against margin. The corner that matters is high margin and mid velocity: the "medium stock" Warren suspects he is under-ordering. Each quadrant carries its share of revenue and gross profit, so the suspicion gets a dollar figure.

DONE WHEN

Warren can name his ten most under-ordered high-margin SKUs, and what the mix shift is worth per year.

The follow-on: the monthly board pack. Once the three panels are live, a monthly job assembles them into the board pack Warren asked for: cash forecast, stock position, margin mix, plus P&L from Xero, delivered to his inbox on the 1st. Post-retreat work, built with human tokens.
