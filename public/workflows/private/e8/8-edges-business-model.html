01The problem

Most AI programs are failing

95%

of enterprise AI pilots deliver no measurable return.

MIT Sloan

Not because the models are weak. Because the company underneath them is not machine readable.

  • The data is scattered

    People, clients, deals, projects, hours, and outcomes live in ten tools that do not talk to each other. There is no single place an agent can read the business from.

  • The workflows are undocumented

    How work actually gets done sits in heads and habits. You cannot automate a process nobody has written down.

  • So the pilots stall

    Teams buy a model, run a demo, and hit a wall the moment it needs real company context. The pilot never becomes production.

02The evidence

The programs that work spend their time on data and workflows

77%

of the work in a successful AI program is data and workflows, not models.

Stanford study of successful AI programs

Everyone is shopping for the last 23%. The winners did the unglamorous 77% first: got their data into one place, then wrote their workflows down.

Data and workflows, 77% Models and tooling, 23%

03Who this is for

Australian companies, startups up to 500 people

Big enough to have the mess. Small enough that one spine can still cover the whole business, and one person can say yes. Australia first, because that is where the relationships already are.

The mess has arrived

Ten tools, none connected. Real clients, real hires, real projects. Enough complexity that scattered data actually costs money.

One person can decide

The founder or CEO is the buyer. No procurement, no security review committee, no eighteen month enterprise cycle.

Nothing is entrenched yet

No warehouse, no seven figure ERP to rip out. Adopting one spine is a decision, not a migration.

Above 500 the sale becomes integration work and the buyer becomes a committee. That is a different business, and not this one yet. Australia is the beachhead, not the ceiling: the product is open source, so it travels on its own.
