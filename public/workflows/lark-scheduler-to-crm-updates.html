Lark Scheduler to CRM Updates

A prospect books a discovery call, the call happens, and then the real work begins: the CRM entry, the transcript, the follow-up email, the next step. Unless someone types it all in, the pipeline quietly falls behind the calendar. This workflow closes that gap with two scheduled agents: one that turns every external booking into a CRM lead before the call, and one that turns every recorded call into a complete CRM record, a drafted follow-up, and a coaching note after it.

Why

The calendar knows things the CRM never hears about

Bookings arrive automatically: a scheduling link drops a meeting on the calendar with a stranger's email on it. Calls get recorded: Lark Minutes produces a full transcript minutes after you hang up. Both systems already hold everything a CRM entry needs. What was missing was the courier.

Done by hand, each discovery call costs 20 to 30 minutes of admin: create the contact, the company, the lead, log the call, paste the summary, write the follow-up. Done by hand, it also gets skipped on busy days, which is exactly when the pipeline is fullest.

The failure mode is silent. Nobody notices an uncaptured lead. The booking happened, the call happened, everyone remembers it went well, and three weeks later there is no record, no next step, and no follow-up ever sent. The cost only shows up as deals that quietly never closed.

6am: lead created

The call happens

The principleThe human stays on the two moments that matter: the call itself, and the send button. Everything between them is courier work, and couriers can be scheduled.

The agents

What each one does, and when

Agent 1

Booking to Lead

RUNS

Every day at 6am, before the workday starts.

WHAT IT DOES

Scans the next 14 days of Lark Calendar for events with an external guest. For each new one it creates the CRM records a lead deserves: the person, their company (inferred from the email domain), the lead itself with status "meeting booked", the inquiry, and the meeting logged as an interaction. Repeat visitors are recognised by email and enriched, never duplicated, and never demoted.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE

A message in Lark listing every new lead with a link to its CRM entry, before the first call of the day. Silence on days with no external bookings.

DONE WHEN

A booking made overnight is a complete CRM lead by breakfast, untouched by a human.

Agent 2

Call to CRM

RUNS

Every day at 6pm, after the day's calls are done.

WHAT IT DOES

Finds the day's recorded external calls in Lark Minutes and, for each one: loads the full transcript into the CRM against the meeting record, writes a structured summary (company snapshot, pain points, BANT, next steps) from the raw conversation rather than the auto-summary, advances the lead truthfully, opens a deal only when the call produced a concrete priced opportunity, and drafts the follow-up email covering exactly what was promised on the call. The draft sits in the mail drafts folder; it never sends itself.

WHAT YOU'LL SEE

One evening message in Lark per day with calls in it: each call's outcome, a link to the CRM entry, a pointer to the waiting draft, and two coaching notes written in the style of a sales coach: one on how the call was run, one on how to play the follow-up. Both grounded in quotes from the transcript, not generic advice.

DONE WHEN

By dinner, every call from that day is a complete CRM record with a follow-up waiting for a single human review and send.

Guardrails

What the agents are not allowed to do

  • Nothing external, ever. No email is sent, no prospect is messaged, no calendar event is touched. The agents write to the CRM and talk to one person: the owner.
  • Drafts are drafts. The follow-up email waits in the drafts folder for a human read and a human send.
  • Leads are not deals. A deal is created only when a call discussed a real, priced opportunity. Nobody gets a pipeline full of phantom value.
  • Idempotent by design. Every write is keyed to the calendar event or the recording token. Re-runs are no-ops, never duplicates.
  • Uncertain means skipped. A booking or recording the agent cannot confidently match is flagged in the report, not guessed at. A wrong record is worse than a missing one.

One dependency: the evening agent needs the call recorded. When a call has no recording, it says so in the report instead of letting the gap pass silently, and the CRM entry gets made by hand the old way.

The coaching layer

The transcript is also a mirror

Because the evening agent reads every transcript anyway, it does one more thing: it critiques. Each call report carries two short notes written from the evidence of the conversation: what the seller did well or missed on this call (talk ratio, discovery depth, whether the pain got a dollar figure, whether the close got a date), and the single highest-leverage move for the next touch with this prospect.

That turns the daily admin loop into a daily practice loop. The CRM catches up on the pipeline; the seller catches up on the craft.

Build it

What this took

  1. A CRM you own. Both agents write to a Supabase database, the same central-database foundation every Edge8 program starts with.
  2. Lark connected once. The command line client, authenticated as the owner, gives the agents calendar, meeting minutes, mail drafts, and messaging.
  3. Two written runbooks. Each agent is a plain-language prompt with its rules, guardrails, and vocabulary spelled out, registered on a schedule. No custom software was built.
  4. One afternoon. From "is there a way to automate this?" to the first live run, including the day's real call landing in the CRM, was a single working session.
