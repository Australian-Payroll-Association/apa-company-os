Improved Scheduling and Tracking

The consulting team’s scheduling layer on the Company OS: scheduling, time entry, resourcing, and client projects and tasks. One system to replace the three surfaces the schedule lives on today (Kantata, a spreadsheet, Adriana’s whiteboard), and to make the two judgments in people’s heads (who has capacity, who can do what) visible.

01

How it works today

The discovery record, in the team’s own terms.

  • Demand. Ad hoc; clients ring up. Deals sit in HubSpot from first contact; the sales cycle runs three days to nine months at the client’s pace. A project moves to Kantata when signed off, or highly likely. On signing the client pays 50% and the project is fully resourced.
  • Scheduling. Each project carries start, end, and a rough capacity estimate, sometimes hours (“100 hours”), sometimes a share of a person’s time (“50%”). Updated every week; end dates hand-shortened when a project is finishing early.
  • Availability. Not pulled from any calendar. The head of advisory’s judgment, resting on real inputs: leave (each consultant enters their own), current projects, maintained end dates. “She’s got three projects but one finishes soon, so she can take another; he can only run one at a time.”
  • Assignment. Kantata holds no skill, territory, or experience data. Adriana carries the map: who can do what, who likes what, how fast. The constraints are real (Emily cannot do a tech procurement; Dave gets every optimisation) and skill is a speed multiplier: two weeks for Dave, five or six for someone learning as they go. Emily is deliberately cross-training others on her usual tasks.
  • Big unsigned projects. Deals needing two or three consultants are added before signing so Ross and Adriana can see a consultant is booked out. Allocated, not billable; billing starts when work starts. Pre-reads and the one-to-two-hour client discovery task are billable.
  • Time entry. Timesheets through Kantata against a 38-hour week, billable / non-billable, by client. Over 100% utilisation genuinely happens. Non-billable books to internal tasks: meetings, collaboration, personal development, travel, new-consultant onboarding.
  • Close-out. Draft report, walkthrough meeting, final report, trim the resourcing, flip to completed. Client notes (“will they come back, be aware of X”) go on the project listing, though not everyone writes them.
The daily frictions, in order of weight

1 · The schedule’s truth is split across Kantata, a spreadsheet, and a whiteboard; the key knowledge lives in two heads.  2 · Project dates, task dates, and resourcing don’t link; one timeline change is three manual updates.  3 · No home for tentative work, so near-certain deals are faked into the confirmed schedule.  4 · When an 8-to-10-week project overruns, nothing proves whether the cause was consultant load or the client sitting on a data request for three months.  5 · Over-utilisation and budget overruns show up only after the fact.

02

What we build

Seven pieces. The first three are the spine; the rest hang off them.

1

One schedule

Projects, tasks, allocations, and leave on a single timeline per consultant, on a rolling six-to-eight-week horizon. Kills the spreadsheet and the whiteboard. Dates are one model: change one, the others follow. (Benchmark: firms planning at least half the workforce eight weeks ahead run about 75% utilisation against 68% for just-in-time staffing.)
spine
2

Tentative projects (the whiteboard, digitised)

A first-class pipeline state: visible in capacity, clearly unconfirmed, never billable, one click to confirm or dissolve. Replaces the “add the big one early” hack. The schedule treats tentative work as binary (in or out, by the written entry rule); the utilisation forecast additionally shows a probability-weighted line from the deal’s pipeline stage, so leadership sees the committed picture and the expected one.
spine
3

Time entry

The Unified Project System’s time_entry, unchanged: fast logging against a 38-hour week, billable or not, by client. No second timesheet. Cadence is the whole game: daily entry recovers material billable hours versus weekly, so the under-10-seconds target is load-bearing.
spine
4

Capability matrix

Per person, per work type: can do fast, can do, learning, cannot do, plus preference. Adriana’s head, written down. Cross-training pairs are planned work, not leftover time. To beat the staleness that kills every skills matrix, it updates at project close-out (who did the work, at what level), not by periodic review.
5

What-if modelling

Win this deal, lose that one, slip a start date; see the ripple on every consultant’s utilisation. The real scenario: two big unsigned projects back to back; if the first slips, does the second move or does someone swap?
6

Early warnings and the variance loop

Person: above 100% utilisation (more than the 38-hour week going to clients). Project: remaining task estimates exceed remaining budget hours (“tasks say 10 hours left, budget says 5”). Plus one report: planned versus actual hours per project type, so each finished project sharpens the next estimate.
7

Client request tracking, with a kickoff SLA

At kickoff, agree and record a client response-time expectation on the project. Log each information request and its days unanswered. Decompose an overrun into our load versus waiting-on-client, measured against the number the client agreed to. Operations tool and commercial defence in one.
Explicitly out

Surveys of any kind, rejected in discovery as too much work. Client memory stays as free-text close-out notes on the project.

how Views

04

Rollout

Three phases, each with a real-world exit test.

PhaseBuildMove on when
1 · One scheduleTimeline view over boards, tasks, staff_assignments, and time_off; linked dates; tentative status.Adriana runs one full weekly scheduling pass in the system, and the whiteboard and spreadsheet are not touched that week.
2 · Judgment made visibleCapability matrix seeded by Adriana; consultant_load view; the two early-warning flags.A new project is assigned using the matrix, and a tracking-over-budget flag fires before the overrun, not after.
3 · Modelling and proofWhat-if scenarios; client_requests and the slip decomposition.A big-deal slip is modelled in the system instead of on the whiteboard, and one overrunning project shows its waiting-on-client days with evidence.

Phase 1 depends on the Unified Project System’s time_entry being live for consulting; the two builds share a pilot team on purpose.

Interim, while the build runs

Kantata stays the working system and its data (allocations, soft bookings, timesheets) is the source to mirror; the team dual-maintains during the transition. All new capability (capability matrix, what-if, client requests, variance) is built in the Company OS only; nothing new is invested in Kantata itself.

05

Decisions to settle

Policy and definitions, not code. Settle them early so nothing gets built twice.

Decision
Who holds the judgment roles. Confirm whether Adriana is the head of advisory or a second person. Availability judgment and assignment judgment may be one seat or two; the permission model and the bus-factor risk both hang on it.
Decision
Work-type list for the capability matrix. Seed from real project types (optimisation, tech procurement, and the rest of Kantata’s list), agreed with Adriana. Keep it under a dozen; this is an assignment aid, not a competency framework.
Decision
When may a tentative project enter the schedule. Today it is “big and highly likely.” Write the rule down (for example: two or more consultants needed, or deal past a named pipeline stage) so the schedule doesn’t fill with wishes.
Data
Get the whiteboard. A photo or sketch of Adriana’s whiteboard, structure not data, before designing the tentative-project screen. Asked for in discovery; not yet received.
Watch
Territories never materialised. Named as a topic at the start of discovery, but skill turned out to be the whole assignment story. Ask once, directly, before assuming they don’t exist.
Watch
Notes adoption. Close-out client notes already have a gap (not everyone writes them). Keep the field one box, on the screen people already use to complete a project.
Watch
Sustained overwork, on the record. The utilisation flag fires at 100% by decision (1 Sep 2026). For the record: the benchmark literature puts the burnout band at sustained utilisation above roughly 85%, and under s62 of the Fair Work Act additional hours beyond 38 must be “reasonable”, with the burden of proof on the employer. If sustained-high weeks become common in the data, revisit the threshold.
