To: Board of Directors, Edge8 From: Office of the CFO Date: 13 July 2026 Classification: Confidential
Re: FY2024–FY2027 revenue and margin outlook, and the shift from growth to profitable growth

Board,

This memo pulls our full financial picture from QuickBooks: three years of actuals, the revenue scenarios through mid-2028, and the one issue that now matters most. The short version is two headlines. First, we built a real revenue engine, tripling from $282K in 2024 to $789K in 2025 and still growing in 2026. Second, and just as important, we did it at roughly breakeven, and the first half of 2026 is our first stretch of genuine operating profit. The strategic question in front of the board is no longer whether we can grow revenue, but whether we can widen the margin as we scale. Everything below builds to that.

1. The three-year picture, from QuickBooks

We built a revenue engine in eighteen months. In 2024 we booked essentially nothing through the first quarter and exited the year near $60K per month, closing at $282K of income. 2025 turned that ramp into a full year of $789K, up 180%. Through June, the last closed month, 2026 income is $436K, running about $80K per month in the most recent quarter, which annualizes near $1.0M, up about 21% over 2025. The tripling you remember is real, and it is a 2024-to-2025 story; the current pace is strong but more measured, which is exactly what you would expect after a from-zero ramp.

2. The margin reality, which is the real headline

The cost side is where the board's attention belongs. Our expense base is dominated by Vietnam contractor labor: $702K of a $788K cost base in 2025, against $789K of revenue. That is the signature of a labor-arbitrage staffing model, and it runs at a thin gross margin (roughly 11% after direct contractor cost in 2025, improving to about 18% in the first half of 2026). The net line is only now turning positive: we lost $22K in 2024, netted $1.8K in 2025, and posted $15K of operating income in the first half of 2026. We are not yet a high-margin business, but the direction is right, and the point stands: at this cost structure, revenue growth scales a low-margin business, and the prize is widening that margin as we grow.

2026 is not a loss; it turned modestly profitable. The closed first half, January to June, shows $436K of income against $421K of cost, for positive operating income of $15K and net income of $34K. The monthly trend improved through the half, from small losses in January and February to positive operating income every month from March on. Costs run near $70K per month, in line with 2025 and rising only with revenue. The direction of travel is the right one.

Read the pattern carefully. Because half of 2026 is already in the books at the current run rate, calendar-2026 growth lands near +22% to +27% even in the Stretch case. The doubling thesis shows up in 2027, where the Stretch case grows about 90% and effectively doubles. So "75% in 2026 and doubling in 2027" is best understood as a forward run-rate ambition; on a reported calendar basis it translates to low-to-mid twenties percent this year and a near-double next year in the aggressive case. But hold these revenue scenarios against Section 2: at today's margins, even the Stretch case throws off almost no profit. The scenarios that will actually move our enterprise value are margin scenarios, not revenue scenarios.

4. Where the value is created

The path to a valuable business is margin, not just revenue. Two levers matter. First, mix: our AI-program and certification revenue carry far better economics than staffing pass-through, and in 2025 AI-program revenue already grew to $133K from $7K the year before. Tilting the revenue mix toward those lines lifts blended margin without adding headcount. Second, staffing markup: a few points of additional margin on $600K-plus of staffing revenue drop straight to the bottom line. The attached model now lets you set the gross-margin assumption directly, defaulted to our recent first-half-2026 level of about 18% rather than a placeholder, so you can see exactly what margin expansion does to contribution.

5. What I am asking the board to approve

  1. Tidy the QuickBooks expense classification so the monthly reports tie to the annual totals (the monthly view currently double-counts), and keep costs entered on time, so month-to-month profitability is legible.
  2. Endorse a margin plan for 2026 to 2027: a target blended gross margin, and the mix shift toward AI-program and certification revenue needed to reach it.
  3. Adopt the Base revenue case (75%/yr forward) as the plan of record, understood as a revenue plan that creates enterprise value only when paired with the margin plan above.

The revenue engine is proven: we tripled the top line in a year and we are still growing. The next chapter is turning that growth profitable. That, not revenue, is the number I want us focused on.
